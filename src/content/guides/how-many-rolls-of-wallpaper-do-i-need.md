**Divide the width you're papering by the roll width to get the number of strips, divide the roll length by the strip length to get strips per roll, and divide the first by the second.** A 12 × 11 ft bedroom with 8 ft walls, one door and an 18 in pattern repeat needs 26 strips; a 20.5 in × 33 ft double roll yields 3 strips, so you need **9 double rolls**.

## Why square feet undercount

The quick method, wall area ÷ roll area, assumes every inch of the roll ends up on the wall. It doesn't. Each roll is cut into full-height strips, and the piece left at the end is usually too short for another strip. With a pattern, each strip must also start at the same point in the design. In the bedroom above, the square-foot method says 7 rolls; strip by strip, it's 9.

## The method

1. **Width to cover.** Add the widths of all walls (for a room, the perimeter) and subtract about 3 ft for each door. Don't subtract windows: the short pieces above and below them still use matched paper.
2. **Strips.** Width (in) ÷ roll width (in), rounded up.
3. **Strip length.** Wall height + 4 in for trimming top and bottom. If the paper has a pattern repeat, round that up to a whole number of repeats.
4. **Strips per roll.** Roll length (in) ÷ strip length, rounded down.
5. **Rolls.** Strips ÷ strips per roll, rounded up.

## Worked example in full

The 12 × 11 ft bedroom:

- Perimeter: 2 × (12 + 11) = 46 ft. Minus one door: **43 ft** = 516 in.
- Strips: 516 ÷ 20.5 = 25.2 → **26**.
- Strip length: 96 + 4 = 100 in. With an 18 in repeat, round up to 6 repeats = **108 in**.
- Strips per roll: 396 ÷ 108 = 3.67 → **3**.
- Rolls: 26 ÷ 3 = 8.67 → **9 double rolls**.

## Check how rolls are sold

In the US, wallpaper is often priced per single roll but sold only in double rolls. European rolls (about 20.9 in × 32.8 ft) are sold singly but hold about as much as a US double roll. Roll sizes vary by maker, so read the label for the actual width and length before counting.

## Buy one extra

Buy at least one extra roll, from the same batch or "run" number. Wallpaper from a later run can differ in shade, and if a strip tears or a wall turns out wider than measured, a matching roll is hard to find later.
