**Most broadloom carpet is made 12 feet wide, and some styles also come 13 1/2 or 15 feet wide. If your room is just over 12 feet in its narrow direction, a wider roll can avoid a seam and save a lot of carpet.** A 14 × 16 ft room takes 38.67 sq yd and 1 seam from a 12 ft roll, but 27.5 sq yd and no seam from a 15 ft roll.

Not every carpet is made in every width. Ask before you choose a style if the room is between 12 and 15 ft wide.

## Why the jump is so big

Carpet pieces always use the full width of the roll. In a room 13 ft wide, a 12 ft roll covers 12 ft with the first piece and needs a whole second piece to cover the last foot. Unless that second piece can be used elsewhere, most of it is waste. An installer may instead run a narrow "fill" strip from the leftover of another cut, which saves carpet but adds a seam; our numbers assume full-width pieces.

## Where seams should go

- **Away from traffic.** Seams wear and can open in walkways, so keep them out of the main path and out of doorways.
- **Toward the light.** A seam running toward the main window is less visible than one running across the light.
- **Same direction.** Every piece must run with its pile in the same direction, or the two sides of a seam will look like different shades.
- **Under furniture** if you can't avoid a seam in the middle of the room.

## Patterned carpet

Patterned and some textured carpets must match across seams, just like wallpaper. That adds a repeat's worth of length to each piece. Ask for the pattern repeat and add it to each cut length when you estimate.
