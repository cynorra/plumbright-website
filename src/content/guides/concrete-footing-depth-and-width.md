**A footing is sized by dividing the load on the post by the soil's bearing capacity, and it must bear on undisturbed soil below the local frost depth (the IRC also sets a minimum of 12 inches below finished grade).** The area comes from the load, the depth comes from your local frost depth, and your building department has the final say on both.

## Frost Line Depth and Footing Thickness Requirements

The International Residential Code (IRC Section R403.1.4) requires footings to extend below the frost line and not less than 12 inches below the finished ground surface. Frost depth varies by town, so use the number your building department gives you; typical ranges are:

- **Northern US Zones:** Frost lines range from 36 to 60 inches deep.
- **Southern US Zones:** Frost lines range from 12 to 24 inches deep.
- **Footing thickness:** A footing should be at least as thick as it projects beyond the post or pier, and the IRC sets a minimum of 6 inches for footings under walls. Deck pier footings are commonly 6 to 10 inches thick; follow your local requirement.

## Step-by-Step Footing Math and Pouring Rules

To size and pour a footing correctly:

1. **Calculate Post Tributary Area:** A deck post supporting half the joist span (6 ft) and half the beam span (8 ft) carries 48 sq ft of deck. At a standard 50 psf total design load (40 live + 10 dead), the post load equals 2,400 lbs.
2. **Determine Footing Area:** In average 2,000 psf soil, required area = 2,400 lbs ÷ 2,000 psf = 1.20 sq ft. An 18-inch round footing provides 1.77 sq ft, exceeding code minimums.
3. **Excavation & Base Prep:** Dig down 6 inches deeper than the frost line, add 6 inches of compacted crushed stone for base drainage, and pour the concrete pad. Place two #4 rebar crosses in the lower third for tensile reinforcement.

## Form Tubes (Sonotubes) vs. Trench-Poured Piers

When building cylindrical post piers above the spread footing, builders use round cardboard form tubes (commonly known by the brand name Sonotube):

- **Trench Pouring (Below Grade):** The spread footing pad at the base can be poured directly into clean, square-dug earth at the bottom of the trench, provided the soil sidewalls are firm and have not collapsed.
- **Form Tubes (Above Grade):** Place the cardboard tube centered on top of the poured footing pad, extending at least 6 inches above final finished grade. Extending the concrete pier above grade protects the wood post from soil moisture, splashback rot, and termite tunnels.
- **Backfill Consolidation:** Backfill soil around the form tube in 6-inch lifts, tamping lightly each time to keep the tube plumb before pouring concrete into the tube.

## Critical Hardware: Post Anchors and Wet-Set Bolts

Avoid burying wooden posts directly in concrete. Wood in contact with concrete can hold moisture and rot out of sight, so most deck builders set a metal post base on the footing instead:

- **Elevated Standoff Bases:** Install galvanized post bases (such as Simpson Strong-Tie ABU or ABA series) that provide a 1-inch standoff plate between the concrete surface and the bottom wood grain. This gap prevents capillary water absorption.
- **Embedded Anchor Bolts:** Wet-set 1/2-inch hot-dipped galvanized J-bolts or threaded anchor rods directly into the center of the wet concrete pier. Leave 1.5 to 2 inches of threaded rod exposed to receive the standoff base and washer nut.
- **Curing Time Before Loading:** Let pier concrete cure before loading it. Many builders wait at least 3 days before setting posts and about a week before loading with beams and joists; follow the mix manufacturer's guidance.
