**Pavers are laid on a screeded layer of coarse bedding sand about 1 inch thick, which works out to about 0.31 cubic yards (roughly 8.3 cubic feet) per 100 sq ft.** The sand sits on a thicker layer of compacted crushed stone base, and that base is where most of the material and work goes.

## How thick should each layer be?

- **Bedding sand: 1 in**, screeded flat and not compacted before the pavers go down. Use coarse, sharp concrete sand, not fine play sand or stone dust that holds water. Thicker sand isn't better: a deep sand layer lets pavers shift and rut.
- **Base: depends on use and soil.** A step-by-step installation guide published by Landscape Management suggests about 3 to 4 in of compacted base for foot traffic, 4 to 5 in for cars and 6 to 8 in for heavy vehicles. Many installers go deeper on clay soil and in freeze-thaw climates, where 6 in under a patio is common.
- **Joint sand:** swept into the joints after the pavers are laid and compacted. Polymeric sand hardens when wetted; coverage depends on joint width and paver size, so use the chart on the bag.

## Worked example: a 14 × 12 ft patio

1. **Bedding sand:** 14 × 12 × 1 ÷ 12 = 14 cu ft = **0.52 yd³**, about 0.7 tons, or 28 bags of 0.5 cu ft. At this size, bags are workable, but a half yard of bulk sand is usually cheaper.
2. **Base:** extend the base 6 in past the patio on every side so the edge pavers are supported: 15 × 13 ft. At 4 in compacted, with 15% extra: **2.77 yd³**, about 4.15 tons.
3. **Excavation:** pavers (often about 2 3/8 in) + 1 in sand + 4 in base = about 7 1/2 in below the finished surface, deeper if your base is thicker.

## Why the base matters more than the sand

The sand only gives the pavers a bed to settle into. Loads spread through the compacted base to the soil below, and a thin or poorly compacted base is the most common reason patios sink and heave. Compact the base in lifts of a few inches with a plate compactor, wetting it slightly, rather than all at once.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Using sand to fix low spots.** Fix the base instead; sand should stay a consistent 1 in.
- **Walking on screeded sand.** Lay pavers from the paver side, working outward.
- **No edge restraint.** Without a restraint along the edges, the pavers and sand slowly spread.
- **Skipping drainage slope.** Build the base with a slight fall away from the house so water runs off.
