**A cubic yard of gravel or crushed stone weighs about 2,600 to 3,000 pounds, or 1.3 to 1.5 tons.** Clean crushed stone like #57 is around 2,700 to 2,800 lb per yard. Crusher run, which includes stone dust, can reach about 3,000 lb. Wet material weighs more than dry.

## Why the same yard can weigh different amounts

- **Fines fill the gaps.** Clean stone has air between the pieces. Crusher run and road base contain stone dust that fills those voids, so a yard of it weighs more and packs harder.
- **Rock type.** Limestone, granite and trap rock have different densities.
- **Moisture.** Sand in particular can hold a lot of water. A yard of wet sand from an uncovered pile can weigh noticeably more than the same sand dry, and you pay for the water if it's sold by weight.
- **Stone size.** A yard of large stone like #3 or #4 has bigger voids and may weigh a little less than a yard of #57.

## Converting yards to tons, and back

Quarries and landscape yards sell by the ton, by the cubic yard, or both. To convert:

- **Tons = cubic yards × lb per yard ÷ 2,000**
- **Cubic yards = tons × 2,000 ÷ lb per yard**

Six cubic yards of crushed stone at 2,700 lb per yard is 6 × 2,700 ÷ 2,000 = **8.1 tons**. Going the other way, a 10-ton load is about **7.41 cubic yards**.

## Worked example: topping a driveway

A gravel driveway 60 ft long and 10 ft wide gets a 4 in top layer of crushed stone.

1. Volume: 60 × 10 × 4 ÷ 12 = 200 cu ft.
2. Cubic yards: 200 ÷ 27 = **7.41 yd³**.
3. Tons: 7.41 × 2,700 ÷ 2,000 = **10 tons**.
4. Order: round up to 7.5 yards, or about 10 tons if the yard sells by weight.

## What can your vehicle carry?

Weight is the reason most people get gravel delivered. A cubic yard of stone is roughly the weight of a small car. Many half-ton pickups have a payload rating well under one ton, which means half a yard of stone can be a full load. Check the payload sticker on the driver's door jamb before you have a yard drop stone in your truck bed, and don't count on the bed's volume: a full bed of gravel would far exceed the payload.

## Bags

A 0.5 cu ft bag of pea gravel weighs about 48 lb at the weight we use, and it takes 54 such bags to make one cubic yard. Bags make sense for a few square feet of decorative stone; for anything past about a yard, bulk delivery is cheaper and much less work.
