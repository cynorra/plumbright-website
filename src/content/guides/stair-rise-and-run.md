**Under the International Residential Code, a riser can be at most 7 3/4 in tall and a tread at least 10 in deep. For a comfortable stair, aim for risers around 7 to 7 1/2 in and treads around 10 1/2 to 11 in, so that twice the riser plus the tread comes to about 24 to 25 in.** Within one flight, risers and treads may not vary by more than 3/8 in.

A stair at the code limits, 7 3/4 in by 10 in, is legal but steep. It's what you find in many basements. Anything you use every day deserves something gentler.

## Three rules of thumb for comfort

Stair designers use simple formulas that match the length of a normal stride:

- **2 × riser + tread = 24 to 25 in.** The oldest rule, attributed to the French architect Blondel.
- **Riser + tread = 17 to 18 in.**
- **Riser × tread = 70 to 75.**

They agree closely in the normal range. Here's what the first rule gives with 2R + T = 25 in:

The 7 1/2 in riser with a 10 in tread passes all three rules and is about the steepest comfortable combination. The 7 in riser with an 11 in tread is a classic, easy stair.

## Worked example: floor to floor of 105 1/2 in

Measure the total rise from finished floor to finished floor: here 105 1/2 in.

1. Divide by a target riser of 7 1/2 in: 105.5 ÷ 7.5 = 14.07, so round up to **15 risers**.
2. Exact riser: 105.5 ÷ 15 = 7.033 in, about **7 1/16 in**.
3. Treads: one fewer than risers, **14**. At 10 1/2 in each, the total run is 147 in, or 12 ft 3 in.
4. Comfort: 2 × 7.03 + 10.5 = **24.57 in**. Right in the comfortable band.

With 14 risers instead, each would be 7 9/16 in, which is legal, and the stair would be shorter by one tread. The [stair calculator](/stair-calculator/) runs these checks for any rise.

## Measure the rise last, and to finished floors

The single most common stair mistake is building to the subfloor. If the top floor gets 3/4 in hardwood and the bottom gets tile on a mortar bed, the finished rise differs from the framing rise, and the first or last step ends up a different height from the rest. That's both a trip hazard and a code violation, since all risers must be within 3/8 in of each other.

<div class="bg-note text-note-foreground my-6 rounded-lg px-4 py-3 text-sm" role="note">Stairs are inspected closely. Confirm riser, tread, headroom, handrail and guard requirements with your local building department before you cut stringers.</div>

## Outdoor and deck stairs

Deck stairs follow the same IRC rules for risers, treads and handrails. Outside, slightly shallower risers and deeper treads (around 6 to 7 in by 11 to 12 in) feel safer in wet weather, and the bottom step needs a solid, level landing rather than soft ground.
