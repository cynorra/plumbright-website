**For a typical floating deck, plan for one concrete deck block every 5 to 6 feet along each main support beam, including blocks at both ends of every beam.** This usually translates to supporting the perimeter beams and any intermediate beams. The exact number depends on the deck's dimensions, the span rating of your chosen lumber (e.g., 2x6, 2x8), and the required gravel base depth for proper drainage, which necessitates reviewing the full calculation guide and material table below.

## Calculating Deck Block Layout and Support Beams

Floating decks, also known as freestanding decks, are low-profile structures that do not attach to a house and rest directly on the ground via concrete deck blocks. These blocks provide a stable, rot-resistant foundation for the deck's main support beams (girders). The primary calculation involves determining how many blocks are needed to adequately support these beams, considering their span capabilities and the overall deck footprint.

The critical factor is the spacing of the blocks along the length of each beam. Standard practice for residential floating decks using common lumber sizes (like 2x6 or 2x8 pressure-treated lumber for beams) dictates a block spacing of approximately 5 to 6 feet on center. This spacing ensures the beams do not sag excessively under load and provides sufficient support for the joists that will rest on top of them. For detailed information on beam sizing, consult our guide on [Deck Beam Span Rules and Sizing: 2x8, 2x10, 2x12](/guides/deck-beam-span-rules-and-sizing-joists/).

**Step-by-Step Calculation:**

1.  **Determine Beam Lengths:** Identify the length of the main support beams that will rest on the deck blocks. For a rectangular deck, these beams typically run parallel to the shorter or longer dimension, depending on your joist layout.
2.  **Calculate Blocks Per Beam:** Divide the length of each beam by your chosen block spacing (e.g., 5 ft or 6 ft). Always round up to the next whole number, then add one more block for the starting point.
    *   `Blocks per Beam = (Beam Length in feet / Desired Spacing in feet) + 1`
    *   *Example:* For a 15-foot beam with 5-foot spacing: (15 / 5) + 1 = 3 + 1 = 4 blocks.
3.  **Determine Number of Beams:** For most floating decks, blocks will support the two perimeter beams (front and back) and at least one or more intermediate beams, depending on the deck's width. A common rule is to space these main support beams every 5 to 6 feet across the width of the deck.
    *   `Number of Beams = (Deck Width in feet / Desired Beam Spacing in feet) + 1`
    *   *Example:* For a 12-foot wide deck with beams spaced every 6 feet: (12 / 6) + 1 = 2 + 1 = 3 beams.
4.  **Calculate Total Deck Blocks:** Multiply the number of blocks per beam by the total number of beams.
    *   `Total Deck Blocks = (Blocks per Beam) * (Number of Beams)`
5.  **Gravel Base and Weed Barrier:**
    *   **Gravel Volume:** Calculate the total footprint area of the deck (Length x Width). Multiply this by the desired gravel depth (typically 4-6 inches, or 0.33 to 0.5 feet).
        *   `Gravel Volume (cu ft) = Deck Length (ft) * Deck Width (ft) * Gravel Depth (ft)`
        *   Convert cubic feet to cubic yards by dividing by 27.
    *   **Weed Barrier Area:** This is simply the total footprint area of the deck.
        *   `Weed Barrier Area (sq ft) = Deck Length (ft) * Deck Width (ft)`
6.  **Waste Allowance:** Always add a 10% waste factor to your total deck block count, gravel volume, and weed barrier area to account for errors, cuts, or unforeseen needs.

## Worked Project Example: 12x16 Floating Deck Foundation

Let's calculate the materials needed for a 12-foot by 16-foot floating deck. We will assume the main support beams run the 16-foot length, and we'll use a 5-foot spacing for the blocks along the beams and a 6-foot spacing for the beams across the 12-foot width. We'll also plan for a 4-inch deep gravel base.

1.  **Determine Beam Lengths:**
    *   Each main support beam will be 16 feet long.

2.  **Calculate Blocks Per Beam:**
    *   Using a 5-foot spacing: `(16 ft / 5 ft) + 1 = 3.2 + 1 = 4.2`. Round up to 5 blocks per beam.

3.  **Determine Number of Beams:**
    *   The deck is 12 feet wide. With beams spaced every 6 feet: `(12 ft / 6 ft) + 1 = 2 + 1 = 3 beams`.
    *   This means we'll have one beam at each 12-foot end and one in the middle.

4.  **Calculate Total Deck Blocks (Raw Count):**
    *   `5 blocks/beam * 3 beams = 15 deck blocks`.

5.  **Apply 10% Waste Factor for Deck Blocks:**
    *   `15 blocks * 1.10 (10% waste) = 16.5`. Round up to 17 deck blocks.

6.  **Calculate Gravel Base Volume:**
    *   Deck footprint: `12 ft * 16 ft = 192 sq ft`.
    *   Gravel depth: 4 inches = `4 / 12 = 0.333 ft`.
    *   Raw gravel volume: `192 sq ft * 0.333 ft = 63.936 cu ft`.

7.  **Apply 10% Waste Factor for Gravel:**
    *   `63.936 cu ft * 1.10 = 70.3296 cu ft`.
    *   To convert to cubic yards (for bulk delivery): `70.3296 cu ft / 27 cu ft/yard = 2.60 cubic yards`.
    *   For more on gravel base considerations, refer to our [Concrete Slab Thickness Guide: Patio, Driveway, Shed, Garage](/guides/concrete-slab-thickness/).

8.  **Calculate Weed Barrier Area:**
    *   Raw weed barrier area: `12 ft * 16 ft = 192 sq ft`.

9.  **Apply 10% Waste Factor for Weed Barrier:**
    *   `192 sq ft * 1.10 = 211.2 sq ft`. Round up to 212 sq ft.

**Summary for a 12x16 Floating Deck:**

*   **Deck Blocks:** 17 blocks
*   **Gravel Base:** 71 cubic feet (or approximately 2.7 cubic yards)
*   **Weed Barrier:** 212 square feet

Remember that these calculations provide a solid estimate. Always double-check your local building codes for specific requirements regarding floating deck foundations and consult the [deck calculator](/deck-calculator/) for further planning assistance.
