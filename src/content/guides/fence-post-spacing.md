**Most wood privacy and picket fences put posts 8 feet apart, on center, because rails are sold in 8 foot lengths and one rail spans exactly one section. Use 6 foot spacing for heavy boards, tall fences, windy or exposed sites and soft soil, or when the fence panels you're buying are made 6 feet wide.**

On a 100 ft fence, closer spacing adds 4 posts and 7 bags of concrete, plus the extra holes to dig. Rail count rises too, but 6 ft rails are often cut from longer stock, which can waste wood unless you buy 12 ft rails and cut each in half.

## Why 8 feet works

- **Lumber lengths.** An 8 ft 2×4 rail spans one section; there's no cutting except at the ends.
- **Fewer holes.** Digging is the hardest part of building a fence. Each post you skip saves a hole, a post and about two bags of concrete.
- **Stiff enough for most fences.** A 2×4 rail over 8 ft carries ordinary pickets on a 4 to 6 ft fence well, especially with three rails on taller fences.

## When 6 feet is better

- **Wind.** A solid privacy fence acts like a sail. Shorter sections spread the load over more posts.
- **Heavy infill.** Thick boards, board-on-board, or horizontal slats add weight and leverage on each post.
- **Soft or wet soil** where posts tend to lean.
- **Prebuilt panels** made 6 ft wide, common in vinyl and some wood panels; the posts must match the panel width exactly.

## Laying out the posts

Spacing is measured center to center. Set the end and corner posts first, stretch a string line between them, then divide the run evenly. If 8 ft doesn't divide the run, make all sections slightly shorter rather than leaving one short section at the end: a 100 ft run is 13 sections, and 100 ÷ 13 = **7.69 ft** each looks better than twelve 8 ft sections and one 4 ft section.

Our [picket guide](/guides/how-many-fence-pickets-do-i-need/) covers the infill, and the [fence calculator](/fence-calculator/) shows posts, rails, pickets and concrete for either spacing.

## Gate posts

A gate hangs its whole weight on one post and swings, so gate posts need more: a deeper hole, a 6×6 or steel post, or both. Never stretch the spacing next to a gate to save a post.
