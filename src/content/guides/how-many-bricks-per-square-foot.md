**A square foot of wall takes about 6.75 modular bricks with standard 3/8 inch mortar joints, according to the Brick Industry Association.** Queen bricks are about 5.5 per square foot, king bricks about 4.55 and utility bricks 3. Add at least 5% for breakage and cutting.

## Where 6.75 comes from

A modular brick is specified as 7 5/8 × 2 1/4 in on its face. Add a 3/8 in joint to the length and height and each brick occupies 8 × 2 5/8 in of wall: 21 square inches. A square foot is 144 square inches, so 144 ÷ 21 = **6.86** bricks. BIA's tables round to 6.75 per square foot, which is the figure most estimators use. Wider joints mean fewer bricks; thinner joints mean more.

## Worked example: brick veneer on a wall

A 40 ft wall, 8 ft tall, with one door and three windows:

1. Gross area: 40 × 8 = 320 sq ft.
2. Openings: 21 + 3 × 15 = 66 sq ft, leaving **254 sq ft**.
3. Bricks: 254 × 6.75 = 1,715, plus 5% = **1801**.
4. Mortar: 16.66 cu ft with 20% extra, about **56 bags** of 80 lb mix.

Real windows vary a lot in size; measure yours rather than relying on the 15 sq ft average.

## What the count doesn't include

- **Rowlocks, soldiers and sills.** Bricks laid on edge or end use more bricks per square foot of face.
- **Corners and returns.** Count both faces of a corner.
- **Double walls.** A two-wythe wall needs twice the bricks plus ties or header courses.
- **Ties, flashing and weeps** for veneer, which the building code governs.

## Buying tips

- **Buy from one production run.** Brick color varies between runs, so order enough at once and blend from several pallets as you lay.
- **Ask about pallet quantities.** Brick is usually sold by the pallet or cube, and partial pallets may cost more per brick.
