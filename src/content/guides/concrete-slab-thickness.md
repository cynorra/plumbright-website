**Most residential slabs are poured 4 inches thick: patios, walkways, shed floors, garage floors and driveways for ordinary cars.** Go to 5 or 6 inches where heavy vehicles, RVs or trailers will park. The International Residential Code sets 3 1/2 inches as the minimum for a concrete floor slab, so 4 inches gives you a little margin.

## What an extra inch costs on a driveway

A single-car driveway 40 ft long and 12 ft wide, with 10% added for waste:

- **4 inches:** 6.52 cubic yards
- **5 inches:** 8.15 cubic yards, 1.63 yd³ more
- **6 inches:** 9.78 cubic yards, 3.26 yd³ more than 4 inches

At this size you'd order ready-mix by the truck, and ready-mix is usually priced per cubic yard, so the jump from 4 to 5 inches adds a quarter to the concrete bill. Whether it's worth it depends on what parks there. For cars on well-compacted base, 4 inches is standard. For a pickup towing a camper, the extra inch is cheap insurance.

## Shed and hot tub pads

A 10 × 12 ft shed pad at 4 inches takes 1.63 cubic yards with 10% waste, or 74 bags of 80 lb mix. That's a lot of bags to mix by hand; past about one cubic yard, many people find a short-load ready-mix delivery or a rented mixer worth the money.

For a hot tub, check the installation manual before you pour. Makers state the slab thickness and flatness they need, and a filled tub with people in it can weigh several tons.

## What goes under the slab

The slab is only as good as what's under it. The usual stack, from the bottom up:

1. **Undisturbed or compacted soil.** Remove topsoil, roots and soft spots. Never pour on loose fill.
2. **Base course.** The IRC calls for a 4 inch base of clean graded sand, gravel or crushed stone under floor slabs, except on well-drained, sandy soils. Many builders use 4 inches of compacted crushed stone under patios too.
3. **Vapor retarder**, for slabs under living or storage space such as a garage or shed, to keep ground moisture out.
4. **Reinforcement.** Wire mesh or rebar on chairs, so it ends up in the middle of the slab rather than lying on the ground.
5. **The slab**, with control joints cut or tooled at regular spacing so cracks happen in the joints.

## Thicken the edges

Slab edges carry more stress than the middle, especially under a shed wall or where a driveway meets the street. A common detail is a thickened edge: the outer 8 to 12 inches of the slab dug deeper, often to 8 inches or more. If you plan one, add its volume separately. A 12 in wide, 4 in deeper edge around a 10 × 12 ft pad adds about 44 ft × 1 ft × 0.33 ft = 14.7 cu ft, roughly half a cubic yard.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Measuring thickness at the forms only.** If the ground dips in the middle, the slab is thicker there and you'll run short. Check the depth across the whole area with a string line.
- **Ordering with no waste.** Uneven subgrade and spillage eat 5 to 10%. Running out mid-pour leaves a cold joint.
- **Skipping the base on clay.** Clay swells and shrinks. A slab poured straight on it cracks sooner.
- **Going thin to save money.** A 3 inch patio uses 25% less concrete than a 4 inch one, but it cracks far more easily and is below the code minimum for floor slabs.
