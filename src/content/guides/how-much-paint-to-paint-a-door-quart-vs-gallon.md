**To paint a standard interior or exterior door (approximately 20 square feet per side), one quart of paint is typically sufficient for two coats on a single side, or one coat on both sides.** However, factors like door style (flat vs. paneled), significant color changes requiring primer, and the inclusion of door jambs and trim often necessitate a full quart per side for two coats, or even a gallon for multiple doors or complex designs to ensure complete coverage and a uniform finish.

## Calculating Door Surface Area for Paint

Accurately determining the surface area of a door is the foundational step for any paint estimation. While a standard door is often approximated at 20 square feet per side, this calculation must account for actual dimensions, both sides, and crucially, the added complexity of paneled designs.

1.  **Measure Door Dimensions:**
    *   Measure the height (H) and width (W) of the door in feet.
    *   *Example:* A common interior door might be 6 feet 8 inches tall (6.67 ft) by 2 feet 8 inches wide (2.67 ft).

2.  **Calculate Area Per Side (Flat Door):**
    *   Area (sq ft) = H (ft) × W (ft)
    *   *Example:* 6.67 ft × 2.67 ft = 17.80 sq ft per side.

3.  **Account for Paneled Doors:**
    *   Paneled doors, with their recessed panels, rails, and stiles, have a significantly greater surface area than flat doors. A general rule of thumb is to add a percentage to the flat surface area:
        *   **Lightly paneled (e.g., 2-panel):** Add 10-15%
        *   **Moderately paneled (e.g., 4-6 panel):** Add 15-25%
        *   **Heavily paneled (e.g., French doors with many small panes, or intricate designs):** Add 25-40%
    *   *Example (Moderately Paneled):* 17.80 sq ft × 1.20 (20% increase) = 21.36 sq ft per side.

4.  **Include Door Edges:**
    *   The top, bottom, and side edges of the door also require paint.
    *   Calculate edge area: (Door Height × Door Thickness) × 2 (for hinge and latch sides) + (Door Width × Door Thickness) × 2 (for top and bottom).
    *   A standard door thickness is 1.375 to 1.75 inches (approx. 0.11 to 0.15 ft).
    *   *Example (1.375" thick):* (6.67 ft × 0.11 ft) × 2 + (2.67 ft × 0.11 ft) × 2 = 1.47 sq ft + 0.59 sq ft = 2.06 sq ft for edges.
    *   For simplicity, many DIYers add 1-2 square feet to the total area per side to account for edges, or simply factor it into the waste allowance.

5.  **Total Surface Area for Painting:**
    *   Total Area = (Area per side + Edge Area adjustment) × Number of Sides × Number of Coats
    *   *Example (Moderately Paneled, both sides, 2 coats):* (21.36 sq ft per side + 1 sq ft edge adjustment) × 2 sides × 2 coats = 22.36 sq ft × 4 = 89.44 sq ft.

6.  **Apply Waste Allowance:**
    *   Always add a 10% waste factor for spills, brush loading, and uneven application.
    *   Total Paintable Area = Total Surface Area × 1.10
    *   *Example:* 89.44 sq ft × 1.10 = 98.38 sq ft.

This final "Total Paintable Area" is the figure you will use to determine how much paint you need based on the paint's coverage rate. For broader project planning, you can also use our [paint calculator](/paint-calculator/) to estimate paint for entire rooms.

## Paint Coverage Rates and Coat Requirements

Paint coverage varies significantly based on paint type, sheen, color, and the porosity of the surface. Most quality paints offer a coverage rate of 350-400 square feet per gallon for a single coat.

*   **Primer:** Essential for unfinished wood, significant color changes (e.g., dark to light), or to ensure better adhesion and a smoother finish. Primer typically covers 200-300 sq ft per gallon, often requiring only one coat. For more details on when to prime, refer to our guide on [Primer and Second Coats: When Paint Coverage Changes](/guides/primer-and-second-coats/).
*   **Number of Coats:**
    *   **New, unprimed, or dark-to-light color change:** 1 coat of primer + 2 coats of paint.
    *   **Light-to-light color change, or previously painted in good condition:** 2 coats of paint.
    *   **Touch-up or very similar color:** 1 coat of paint might suffice, but 2 is recommended for durability and uniformity.

## Worked Project Example: Painting a Paneled Front Door

Let's calculate the paint needed for a single exterior front door that is moderately paneled, requires a significant color change (dark to light), and will be painted on both sides, including the edges.

**Door Specifications:**
*   Height: 80 inches (6.67 ft)
*   Width: 36 inches (3 ft)
*   Thickness: 1.75 inches (0.146 ft)
*   Style: Moderately paneled (add 20% to flat surface area)
*   Desired Finish: 1 coat of primer, 2 coats of exterior paint
*   Paint Coverage: 375 sq ft/gallon (93.75 sq ft/quart)
*   Primer Coverage: 250 sq ft/gallon (62.5 sq ft/quart)

**Step 1: Calculate Flat Surface Area per Side**
*   Area = Height × Width = 6.67 ft × 3 ft = 20.01 sq ft

**Step 2: Adjust for Paneled Design**
*   Paneled Area = 20.01 sq ft × 1.20 (20% increase) = 24.01 sq ft per side

**Step 3: Calculate Edge Area**
*   Edges = (H × T × 2) + (W × T × 2) = (6.67 ft × 0.146 ft × 2) + (3 ft × 0.146 ft × 2)
*   Edges = (1.95 sq ft) + (0.88 sq ft) = 2.83 sq ft
*   For simplicity, we'll add this to the total area for each side.

**Step 4: Calculate Total Area for Primer (Both Sides)**
*   Area per side (including panels and edges) = 24.01 sq ft + 2.83 sq ft = 26.84 sq ft
*   Total Primer Area = 26.84 sq ft/side × 2 sides × 1 coat = 53.68 sq ft
*   Add 10% waste: 53.68 sq ft × 1.10 = 59.05 sq ft

**Step 5: Calculate Total Area for Paint (Both Sides)**
*   Total Paint Area = 26.84 sq ft/side × 2 sides × 2 coats = 107.36 sq ft
*   Add 10% waste: 107.36 sq ft × 1.10 = 118.09 sq ft

**Step 6: Determine Material Quantities**

*   **Primer:**
    *   Needed: 59.05 sq ft
    *   Primer coverage per quart: 62.5 sq ft
    *   Quarts of Primer = 59.05 sq ft / 62.5 sq ft/quart = 0.94 quarts
    *   **Purchase: 1 Quart of Primer**

*   **Paint:**
    *   Needed: 118.09 sq ft
    *   Paint coverage per quart: 93.75 sq ft
    *   Quarts of Paint = 118.09 sq ft / 93.75 sq ft/quart = 1.26 quarts
    *   **Purchase: 2 Quarts of Paint** (or 1 Gallon if you prefer to have extra for touch-ups or other small projects, as a gallon is 4 quarts).

This detailed breakdown ensures all surfaces are accounted for, minimizing the risk of running out of paint mid-project. For larger projects involving multiple doors or rooms, understanding how to calculate paint for a 15x15 room can provide a broader perspective on material needs.
