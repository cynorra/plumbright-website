**For brick laying, Type N mortar typically uses a 1:1:6 ratio (1 part Portland cement, 1 part hydrated lime, 6 parts sand by volume) for non-load-bearing exterior walls, while Type S mortar uses a 1:0.5:4.5 ratio for load-bearing or below-grade applications requiring higher strength.** Understanding these precise volumetric proportions and accounting for project-specific factors like wall dimensions, joint thickness, and a crucial 10% waste allowance is essential for accurate material estimation, as detailed in the comprehensive table and calculations below.

## Understanding Mortar Types and Ratios

Mortar is the binding agent that holds bricks together, forming a strong, durable masonry structure. It consists primarily of Portland cement, hydrated lime, and sand, mixed with water. The specific proportions of these dry ingredients determine the mortar's strength, flexibility, and workability, leading to different "types" suitable for various applications. For brick laying, Type N and Type S are the most common choices for site-mixed mortar.

*   **Portland Cement:** Provides the primary strength and binding properties.
*   **Hydrated Lime:** Improves workability, water retention, and flexibility, reducing cracking.
*   **Sand:** Acts as the aggregate, providing bulk, reducing shrinkage, and contributing to compressive strength.

The ratios are always expressed as Cement:Lime:Sand by volume.

### Type N Mortar (Normal Strength)

Type N mortar is a general-purpose mortar suitable for most above-grade, non-load-bearing exterior walls and interior applications. It offers good workability and moderate compressive strength (typically 750 psi or 5.2 MPa). Its balanced composition makes it ideal for chimneys, garden walls, and veneers where extreme structural demands are not present.

*   **Volumetric Ratio:** 1 part Portland cement : 1 part hydrated lime : 6 parts sand

### Type S Mortar (Structural Strength)

Type S mortar is a higher-strength mortar designed for applications requiring superior bonding and compressive strength (typically 1800 psi or 12.4 MPa). It is recommended for load-bearing walls, below-grade masonry, foundations, and areas subjected to high wind or seismic activity. The reduced lime content and increased cement content contribute to its enhanced structural properties.

*   **Volumetric Ratio:** 1 part Portland cement : 0.5 parts hydrated lime : 4.5 parts sand

## Calculating Mortar Volume and Material Quantities

Accurately estimating mortar quantities begins with determining the total number of bricks required for your project, as mortar consumption is often calculated per 1000 bricks. For standard bricks (approximately 7 5/8" L x 2 1/4" H x 3 5/8" W) with typical 3/8-inch mortar joints, approximately 7 cubic feet of mixed mortar are needed per 1000 bricks.

Here's a step-by-step breakdown for calculating your material needs:

1.  **Determine Total Bricks:**
    *   Calculate the total square footage of your wall(s): `Wall Length (ft) x Wall Height (ft) = Total Wall Area (sq ft)`.
    *   For standard bricks with 3/8-inch mortar joints, approximately 6.75 bricks cover 1 square foot.
    *   `Total Bricks = Total Wall Area (sq ft) x 6.75 bricks/sq ft`. Round up to the nearest whole brick.
    *   For a precise brick count, use our [brick calculator](/brick-calculator/).

2.  **Calculate Total Mortar Volume:**
    *   `Total Mortar Volume (cu ft) = (Total Bricks / 1000) x 7 cu ft/1000 bricks`.

3.  **Apply Mortar Ratio to Volume:**
    *   Based on your chosen mortar type (N or S), use the volumetric ratios to determine the required cubic feet of cement, lime, and sand.
    *   **For Type N (1:1:6 = 8 total parts):**
        *   `Cement (cu ft) = (1/8) x Total Mortar Volume (cu ft)`
        *   `Lime (cu ft) = (1/8) x Total Mortar Volume (cu ft)`
        *   `Sand (cu ft) = (6/8) x Total Mortar Volume (cu ft)`
    *   **For Type S (1:0.5:4.5 = 6 total parts):**
        *   `Cement (cu ft) = (1/6) x Total Mortar Volume (cu ft)`
        *   `Lime (cu ft) = (0.5/6) x Total Mortar Volume (cu ft)`
        *   `Sand (cu ft) = (4.5/6) x Total Mortar Volume (cu ft)`

4.  **Convert to Bags and Cubic Feet:**
    *   **Portland Cement:** 1 bag (94 lb) = 1 cubic foot.
    *   **Hydrated Lime:** 1 bag (50 lb) = 1.25 cubic feet.
    *   **Sand:** Measured in cubic feet.
    *   Divide the calculated cubic feet of cement and lime by their respective bag yields, then round up to the nearest whole bag for purchase.

5.  **Add Waste Allowance:**
    *   Always add a 10% waste allowance to your final material quantities to account for spillage, mixing errors, and variations in joint thickness.
    *   `Final Material Quantity = Calculated Quantity x 1.10`.

## Worked Project Example: Garden Wall

Let's estimate the materials for a garden wall that is 20 feet long and 3 feet high, using standard bricks and Type N mortar. The wall will be built on a proper footing, as detailed in guides like our Concrete Footing Depth and Width: Frost Line & Load Sizing Rules.

1.  **Determine Total Bricks:**
    *   Wall Area = 20 ft (length) x 3 ft (height) = 60 sq ft.
    *   Total Bricks = 60 sq ft x 6.75 bricks/sq ft = 405 bricks.

2.  **Calculate Total Mortar Volume:**
    *   Total Mortar Volume = (405 bricks / 1000) x 7 cu ft/1000 bricks = 0.405 x 7 = 2.835 cu ft.

3.  **Apply Type N Mortar Ratio (1:1:6 = 8 total parts):**
    *   Cement (cu ft) = (1/8) x 2.835 cu ft = 0.354 cu ft
    *   Lime (cu ft) = (1/8) x 2.835 cu ft = 0.354 cu ft
    *   Sand (cu ft) = (6/8) x 2.835 cu ft = 2.126 cu ft

4.  **Convert to Bags and Cubic Feet (before waste):**
    *   **Cement:** 0.354 cu ft / 1 cu ft/bag = 0.354 bags. Round up to **1 bag (94 lb)**.
    *   **Lime:** 0.354 cu ft / 1.25 cu ft/bag = 0.283 bags. Round up to **1 bag (50 lb)**.
    *   **Sand:** 2.126 cu ft. Round up to **3 cu ft**.

5.  **Add 10% Waste Allowance:**
    *   **Cement:** 1 bag x 1.10 = 1.1 bags. Purchase **2 bags (94 lb)**.
    *   **Lime:** 1 bag x 1.10 = 1.1 bags. Purchase **2 bags (50 lb)**.
    *   **Sand:** 3 cu ft x 1.10 = 3.3 cu ft. Purchase **4 cu ft**.

For this 20 ft x 3 ft garden wall, you would need to purchase 2 bags of 94 lb Portland cement, 2 bags of 50 lb hydrated lime, and 4 cubic feet of sand.

## Essential Tips for Mixing and Application

Proper mixing and application are as crucial as accurate material estimation for a successful bricklaying project.

*   **Dry Mix First:** Always combine all dry ingredients (cement, lime, sand) thoroughly in a wheelbarrow or mixer before adding water. This ensures a consistent blend and uniform strength throughout the mortar.
*   **Add Water Gradually:** Slowly add clean water while mixing, aiming for a workable, plastic consistency. The mortar should be stiff enough to hold its shape on the trowel but pliable enough to spread easily. Avoid making it too wet, as this weakens the mortar and can lead to excessive shrinkage.
*   **Work in Batches:** Mix only as much mortar as you can use within 1 to 1.5 hours. Mortar begins to set once water is added, and remixing old, stiffened mortar by adding more water (re-tempering) significantly reduces its strength.
*   **Joint Thickness:** Maintain consistent 3/8-inch mortar joints for both horizontal (bed joints) and vertical (head joints). This not only looks professional but also ensures the structural integrity of the wall.
*   **Tooling:** Tool the mortar joints when they are thumbprint hard. This compacts the mortar, improves its weather resistance, and creates a finished appearance.
*   **Waste Allowance:** As demonstrated, always factor in a 10% waste allowance. This buffer prevents costly delays and extra trips to the store, ensuring you have enough material to complete the job without interruption.
