**Add up the length of every eave and rake, divide by the usable length of one piece (a 10 ft piece covers about 9.75 ft once a 3 in lap is taken out), add 10% for cuts and round up.** A 40 × 25 ft gable roof at 6/12 has about 136 linear feet of edge and needs 16 pieces.

## Measuring the edges

- **Eaves** are the low, horizontal edges where water leaves the roof. Add up the full length of each.
- **Rakes** are the sloped edges of the gable ends. A rake is longer than the horizontal distance beneath it, by the pitch multiplier: √(1 + (rise ÷ 12)²). For 6/12 that is √1.25 = 1.118.
- **Hips and ridges** do not take drip edge.

## The formula

1. **Total length (LF)** = all eaves + all rakes.
2. **Usable length per piece** = 10 ft − lap. The IRC calls for joints that are lapped at least 2 in; this guide uses 3 in, so each piece adds 9.75 ft to the run.
3. **Pieces** = LF × 1.10 ÷ 9.75, rounded up. The 10% covers cuts at corners, miters and mistakes.

Each separate run wastes a part of its last piece, so a roof with many short runs needs a few more pieces than one with a few long runs.

## Worked example: 40 × 25 ft gable roof at 6/12

1. **Eaves:** two 40 ft eaves = **80 LF**.
2. **Rakes:** each gable end has two sloped edges, each as long as half the 25 ft width times the pitch multiplier: 12.5 × 1.118 = 13.98 ft. Four rake edges = **55.9 LF**.
3. **Total:** 80 + 55.9 = **135.9 LF**.
4. **Usable per piece:** 10 − 0.25 = 9.75 ft, so 135.9 ÷ 9.75 = 13.94 pieces.
5. **Waste:** 13.94 × 1.10 = 15.33, rounded up to **16 pieces**.

## Installation notes

- **Eaves first, under the underlayment.** Water that gets under the shingles then runs out over the drip edge. **Rakes go over the underlayment.** This is the IRC order (R905.2.8.5).
- **Lap and fasten.** Overlap joints at least 2 in. Fasten with corrosion-resistant roofing nails; the IRC allows a maximum spacing of 12 in on center. Do not overdrive nails.
- **Materials.** Aluminum and galvanized steel are the usual choices. Do not mix dissimilar metals where they touch.
- **Local rules.** Drip edge is required by the IRC at eaves and rakes of shingle roofs, but local amendments vary, so check yours.
