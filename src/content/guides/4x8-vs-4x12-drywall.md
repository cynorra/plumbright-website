**Use 4 × 12 sheets when you have help and room to carry them: they cover 50% more per sheet and leave fewer butt joints, which are the hardest seams to finish flat. Use 4 × 8 sheets when you're working alone, carrying board up stairs or into small rooms, or hanging ceilings without a lift.** The material cost per square foot is usually about the same; the difference is labor and finish quality.

## Why butt joints matter

The long edges of drywall are tapered, so when two sheets meet along those edges, there's a shallow recess to hold tape and compound. The short ends aren't tapered. Where two ends meet, a butt joint, the tape and compound sit on top of a flat surface and have to be feathered out wide, often 16 to 24 in, to hide the hump. Fewer butt joints means less compound, less sanding and flatter walls.

Walls up to 12 ft long can be hung with no butt joints at all using 4 × 12s. On a 16 ft wall, 4 × 8s give a butt joint in the middle of each course, while 4 × 12s give one joint plus a 4 ft piece. Many hangers stagger the joints between the top and bottom course so they don't line up.

## The case for 4 × 8

- **Weight.** A 4 × 12 sheet weighs 50% more than a 4 × 8 of the same board. Lifting one onto a ceiling without a drywall lift is a two-person job at least.
- **Access.** 12 ft sheets don't turn tight stair landings or fit in many basements, and they don't fit in most pickup beds or SUVs. Delivery may be required.
- **Small rooms.** In closets, bathrooms and short walls, a 12 ft sheet gets cut down anyway.
- **Availability.** Every store stocks 4 × 8; 4 × 12 and specialty sizes are less certain.

## The case for 4 × 12

- **Fewer butt joints** and better-looking walls, especially under paint with any sheen.
- **Faster hanging** once the sheets are in the room: fewer pieces, fewer screws per square foot of edge.
- **Less finishing.** Fewer seams means fewer hours of taping and sanding, which is where most of the labor goes.

## Other sizes worth knowing

- **4 × 10** fits 10 ft walls hung vertically with no horizontal seam, and covers 9 ft walls with a short cut.
- **54 in wide** board (4 1/2 ft) is made for 9 ft walls hung horizontally: two courses, no strip.
- **4 × 9** is sometimes stocked for 9 ft walls hung vertically.

If you hire a pro to finish, ask which size they prefer; most will choose the longest sheet that fits the room.
