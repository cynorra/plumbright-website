**Building codes mandate that exterior concrete footings must extend at least 12 inches below the local frost line depth and measure at least twice the width of the supported post or wall (typically 16 to 24 inches square).** Sizing your footings below the frost penetration line prevents freeze-thaw heaving, while the surface area distributes building loads across undisturbed native soil without settling.

## Frost Line Depth and Footing Thickness Requirements

The International Residential Code (IRC Section R403) requires footings to bear on undisturbed soil below the regional frost heave zone:

- **Northern US Zones:** Frost lines range from 36 to 60 inches deep.
- **Southern US Zones:** Frost lines range from 12 to 24 inches deep.
- **Footing Thickness:** The concrete footing thickness must equal or exceed the width of the footing projection (minimum 6 inches thick for residential decks and light outbuildings; 8 to 12 inches for residential additions).

## Step-by-Step Footing Math and Pouring Rules

To size and pour a footing correctly:

1. **Calculate Post Tributary Area:** A deck post supporting half the joist span (6 ft) and half the beam span (8 ft) carries 48 sq ft of deck. At a standard 50 psf total design load (40 live + 10 dead), the post load equals 2,400 lbs.
2. **Determine Footing Area:** In average 2,000 psf soil, required area = 2,400 lbs ÷ 2,000 psf = 1.20 sq ft. An 18-inch round footing provides 1.77 sq ft, exceeding code minimums.
3. **Excavation & Base Prep:** Dig down 6 inches deeper than the frost line, add 6 inches of compacted crushed stone for base drainage, and pour the concrete pad. Place two #4 rebar crosses in the lower third for tensile reinforcement.
