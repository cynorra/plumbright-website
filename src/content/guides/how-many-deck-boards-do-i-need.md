**Divide the deck's width (in inches) by the board width plus the gap to get the number of rows, then work out how many boards each row needs.** A 14 × 12 ft deck with 5.5 in boards and 3/16 in gaps has 26 rows; each 14 ft row is one 16 ft board, so you need **26 boards, 28 with 5% extra**.

## Why count rows instead of dividing the area?

Dividing deck area by board area gives a number, but it ignores how boards are actually cut. Every row must be covered end to end, and a 2 ft offcut from a 16 ft board is often too short to use anywhere. Counting rows and planning each row's boards gives a number you can actually build with.

## Step by step

1. **Rows.** Width in inches ÷ (board width + gap), rounded up. For a 12 ft (144 in) deck: 144 ÷ (5.5 + 0.1875) = 25.3, so **26 rows**.
2. **Boards per row.** If a board covers the full length, it's one board per row. If not, the row needs a joint, and the joint must land on a joist.
3. **Reuse offcuts.** A long offcut from one row can finish another. A 16 ft board cut to 12 ft leaves 4 ft, which can be the short piece in a row that needs one.
4. **Add extra.** About 5% for warped or split boards and a miscut. More for diagonal decking.

Board lengths that divide evenly into the deck length waste almost nothing: 20 ft boards cut in half for a 10 ft deck, 8, 12 or 16 ft boards for a 24 ft deck. A 14 ft deck is the awkward one. 16 ft boards give one clean, joint-free board per row but cut 2 ft off every board, about 14% waste. 12 ft boards waste only about 2%, but every row then has a joint with a short 2 ft piece, which looks busy and needs extra blocking. Many builders choose the 16 footers anyway. Lowest waste isn't always the best-looking deck.

## Plan the joints

Butt joints where two boards meet must fall on a joist, and many builders add a second joist or blocking there so each board end gets full bearing. Staggering joints across rows looks better than lining them up. Some decks use a "picture frame" border or a divider board instead, so boards run full length between borders.

## Don't forget

- **Fascia and stair treads.** Rim joist fascia and stair treads are extra boards on top of the decking count.
- **Fasteners.** Two screws per board at every joist. A 14 × 12 ft deck with joists 16 in apart has 11 joists and 26 rows: about 572 screws.
- **Hidden fastener clips** for grooved composite boards replace face screws; count clips per the maker's instructions.
- **Gap.** Wood decking shrinks as it dries, so many builders install pressure-treated boards tight and let gaps open. Composite boards need the gap the manufacturer specifies.
