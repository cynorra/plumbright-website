To find the area of an L-shaped room, **split it into two rectangles, find the area of each and add them together.** A room that is 14 ft on its long side, with a 6 × 5 ft corner cut out, measures 14 × 12 − 6 × 5 = 138 sq ft. Either method below gives the same answer; pick whichever needs fewer measurements in your room.

1. Draw a rough sketch of the room. It does not need to be to scale.
2. Draw a line that splits the L into two rectangles. Extend one of the inside walls across the room.
3. Measure the length and width of each rectangle.
4. Multiply length × width for each one.
5. Add the results.

For the room above: rectangle A is 8 × 12 = 96 sq ft, rectangle B is 6 × 7 = 42 sq ft. **Total: 138 sq ft.**

## Method 2: measure the big box, subtract the notch

Imagine the room filled out into a full rectangle, then take away the missing corner. For the same room, the outer box is 14 × 12 = 168 sq ft. The missing corner is 6 × 5 = 30 sq ft. **168 − 30 = 138 sq ft**, the same as before.

This method is quicker when the notch is small, such as a closet or a chimney chase that juts into the room.

## Measuring in feet and inches

Tape measures read in feet and inches, but the math needs decimal feet. Divide the inches by 12 and add them to the feet: 11 ft 6 in is 11 + 6 ÷ 12 = **11.5 ft**.

## What to include and what to leave out

- **Closets:** include them if you are tiling or putting flooring in them.
- **Cabinets and islands:** leave them out for tile, unless they are coming out.
- **Under appliances:** include the space under a dishwasher or range, so the appliance can slide out at floor height.
- **Doorways:** include the floor in the doorway up to the middle of the door, where the transition strip sits.
- **Tubs and showers:** leave them out of the floor area. Measure tile on the walls around them as a separate area.

## Tips for an accurate measurement

- Measure at floor level. Walls are rarely perfectly straight, and baseboard height is where the flooring meets them.
- Measure each wall twice, at both ends. If the numbers differ, use the larger one.
- Round up to the next inch, never down.
- Write every measurement on your sketch as you take it.
