**A standard 8 × 8 × 16 inch concrete block covers 8 × 16 = 128 square inches of wall including its mortar joint, so a wall takes 144 ÷ 128 = 1.125 blocks per square foot.** Another way to count: 1 1/2 courses per foot of height and 3/4 of a block per foot of length. Add about 5% for breakage and cuts.

## Count by courses

Nominal block sizes include the 3/8 inch mortar joint: the actual block is 7 5/8 × 15 5/8 in. So every course is 8 in tall and every block takes 16 in of length.

- **Courses** = wall height (in) ÷ 8, rounded up
- **Blocks per course** = wall length (in) ÷ 16, rounded up
- **Blocks** = courses × blocks per course

Counting by courses rounds each course up to a whole block, which is closer to how a wall is actually built; counting by area with 5% added gives a similar total for most walls.

## Worked example: a block foundation for a shed

A 12 × 10 ft shed on a block perimeter wall, 2 ft (3 courses) tall:

1. Wall length: 2 × (12 + 10) = **44 ft**.
2. Area: 44 × 2 = 88 sq ft × 1.125 = 99 blocks, plus 5% = **104 blocks**.
3. Mortar: about **10 bags** of 80 lb mix.
4. Corners: each corner block shows two faces, so corners don't need extra blocks, but half blocks or corner blocks keep the joints staggered; ask for a few.

## Other block sizes

- **4 in, 6 in, 10 in and 12 in wide blocks** usually share the 8 × 16 in face, so the count per square foot is the same; only the weight and price change.
- **Half blocks** (8 × 8 face) and **cap blocks** (2 to 4 in thick) are counted separately: caps by wall length ÷ 16 in.
- **Retaining wall blocks** have their own sizes and setbacks; use the maker's count per square foot of face.

<div class="bg-note text-note-foreground my-6 rounded-lg px-4 py-3 text-sm" role="note">Block walls that hold back soil, carry a building or rise over about 4 ft usually need a footing, reinforcement and grouted cores sized by code or an engineer. Check with your building department.</div>
