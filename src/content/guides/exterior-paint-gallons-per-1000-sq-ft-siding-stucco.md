**For every 1,000 square feet of exterior surface, expect to need approximately 2.7 to 4 gallons of paint for a single coat on smooth siding, or 4.5 to 6 gallons for a single coat on porous stucco, before accounting for a second coat or waste.** Actual paint consumption varies significantly based on surface texture, porosity, existing color, and the number of coats applied, making a detailed calculation essential.

## Calculating Exterior Paint Coverage for Diverse Surfaces

Estimating the correct amount of exterior paint requires more than a simple square footage calculation; it demands an understanding of how different surface materials absorb and hold paint. Smooth, non-porous surfaces like pre-primed cedar lap siding or vinyl siding will yield higher coverage rates per gallon compared to highly porous and textured materials such as unprimed stucco, brick, or rough-sawn wood. The key is to determine the total paintable area and then divide it by the specific coverage rate of your chosen paint on your particular surface type.

To begin, calculate the total square footage of the exterior walls. Measure the length of each wall and multiply it by its height. Sum these values for all walls. Next, subtract the square footage of any non-paintable areas, such as windows, doors, and garage doors. A general rule of thumb for subtracting these openings is to measure their width and height, multiply them, and subtract the total from your wall area. For instance, a standard 3-foot by 7-foot door is 21 square feet, and a 3-foot by 4-foot window is 12 square feet.

The fundamental formula for determining paint quantity is:

`Total Gallons Needed = (Total Paintable Square Footage / Paint Coverage Rate per Gallon) * Number of Coats * Waste Factor`

For example, if a wall measures 20 feet long by 10 feet high, its area is 200 square feet. If there is a 3x4 foot window (12 sq ft), the paintable area becomes 188 square feet. If the paint covers 350 square feet per gallon and two coats are desired, the calculation for this section would be: (188 sq ft / 350 sq ft/gallon) * 2 coats = 1.07 gallons. Adding a 10% waste factor (for spills, roller saturation, touch-ups) brings the total to 1.07 * 1.10 = 1.18 gallons for that specific wall section. This methodical approach ensures accuracy, especially when dealing with varying surface types on a single structure. The porosity and texture of an exterior surface are the primary determinants of paint coverage. Smooth, sealed surfaces allow paint to spread further, while rough, absorbent materials require more paint to achieve adequate coverage and color uniformity. New, unprimed surfaces, especially masonry like stucco or concrete, will often require a dedicated primer coat to seal the surface and ensure proper paint adhesion and coverage for the finish coats. For details on primer application, refer to our guide on [primer and second coats](/guides/primer-and-second-coats/).

## Project Example: Painting a 2,500 Sq Ft Home with Mixed Siding and Stucco

Consider a single-story home with a total exterior paintable area of 2,500 square feet. This home features 1,800 square feet of smooth fiber cement lap siding and 700 square feet of moderately rough stucco. The project requires two coats of paint for both surfaces.

**Step 1: Calculate Paint for Smooth Siding Area**
*   **Siding Area:** 1,800 sq ft
*   **Assumed Coverage Rate (Smooth Siding):** 375 sq ft per gallon (from table, within 350-400 range)
*   **Paint needed for one coat:** 1,800 sq ft / 375 sq ft/gallon = 4.8 gallons
*   **Paint needed for two coats:** 4.8 gallons * 2 = 9.6 gallons
*   **Add 10% waste factor:** 9.6 gallons * 1.10 = 10.56 gallons

**Step 2: Calculate Paint for Moderately Rough Stucco Area**
*   **Stucco Area:** 700 sq ft
*   **Assumed Coverage Rate (Moderately Rough Stucco):** 225 sq ft per gallon (from table, within 200-250 range for smooth/sealed, but we'll use a slightly lower end for "moderately rough" to be safe, as it's not "highly porous")
*   **Paint needed for one coat:** 700 sq ft / 225 sq ft/gallon = 3.11 gallons
*   **Paint needed for two coats:** 3.11 gallons * 2 = 6.22 gallons
*   **Add 10% waste factor:** 6.22 gallons * 1.10 = 6.84 gallons

**Step 3: Calculate Total Paint Required**
*   **Total Paint:** 10.56 gallons (siding) + 6.84 gallons (stucco) = 17.40 gallons

**Step 4: Determine Number of 1-Gallon Cans**
*   Since paint is sold in full gallons, round up to the nearest whole gallon.
*   **Total Gallons to Purchase:** 18 gallons

This detailed breakdown ensures that enough paint is purchased for the entire project, accounting for the specific characteristics of each surface and providing a buffer for unforeseen circumstances. Always review the specific coverage rates listed on your chosen paint can, as these can vary slightly by manufacturer and product line.
