**A 12 × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls takes 12 sheets of 4 × 8 drywall for the walls and 5 for the ceiling: 17 sheets in total.** That includes one door and one window subtracted and 10% added for cutting waste. With 4 × 12 sheets it's 12.

## The calculation

1. **Wall area:** 4 walls × 12 ft × 8 ft = 384 sq ft.
2. **Subtract openings:** a door (about 21 sq ft) and a window (about 15 sq ft) leave **348 sq ft**.
3. **Add 10% waste:** 348 × 1.10 = 382.8 sq ft.
4. **Divide by the sheet:** a 4 × 8 sheet is 32 sq ft. 382.8 ÷ 32 = 11.96 → **12 sheets**.
5. **Ceiling:** 144 sq ft × 1.10 ÷ 32 = 4.95 → **5 sheets**.

## Check it sheet by sheet

The area method is quick, but drywall gets hung in whole sheets, so it's worth checking against a layout. Sheets are usually hung horizontally, two rows high on an 8 ft wall:

- Each 12 ft wall is 1 1/2 sheets long and 2 sheets high: **3 sheets of 4 × 8** per wall, or exactly **2 sheets of 4 × 12**.
- Four walls: **12 sheets** of 4 × 8, or **8 sheets** of 4 × 12, before any openings.
- Ceiling: 12 × 12 ft is 4.5 sheets of 4 × 8, so **5 sheets**, or 3 sheets of 4 × 12.

The layout gives 12 + 5 = 17 sheets of 4 × 8, the same as the area method. In the layout, full sheets run right over the door and window and the openings are cut out afterward; those cutouts are the waste the 10% allowance pays for. That's why the area method subtracts openings and still adds 10%.

## Wall height changes the best sheet

With 9 ft walls, two rows of 4 ft sheets leave a 1 ft gap. You can fill it with strips, but that adds a seam the full length of the wall. Two better options: hang 4 × 9 or 4 × 10 sheets vertically, or use 54 in wide board, which is made for 9 ft walls hung horizontally in two rows. With 10 ft walls, vertical 4 × 10s avoid a third row.

## The rest of the shopping list

For the 8 ft room with the ceiling, 17 sheets of 4 × 8:

- **Screws:** about 28 per wall sheet and 35 per ceiling sheet, so roughly **511**. 1 1/4 in coarse-thread screws suit 1/2 in board on wood studs.
- **Joint compound:** about **4.9 gallons**, so 2 pails of premixed all-purpose compound.
- **Paper tape:** about **201 ft**, one 500 ft roll, plus metal or paper-faced corner bead for outside corners.

## Ceiling board is different

Many builders use 1/2 in sag-resistant board or 5/8 in board on ceilings, especially with joists 24 in apart. Regular 1/2 in board can sag between framing members over time. Check what your framing and local code call for before buying the ceiling sheets with the wall sheets.
