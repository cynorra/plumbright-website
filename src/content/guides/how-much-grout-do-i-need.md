**Grout volume = joint length × joint width × tile thickness. For 12 × 12 in tile, 3/8 in thick with 1/8 in joints, that's about 9 lb of sanded grout per 100 sq ft, so a 25 lb bag covers roughly 267 sq ft.** Small tiles and wide joints need much more: a 2 × 2 in mosaic takes about four times as much grout per square foot as 12 × 12 tile.

## The formula

Grout fills the joints to the depth of the tile. Per square foot of floor:

1. **Joint length** (in inches) = 144 × (tile width + tile length) ÷ (tile width × tile length). For 12 × 12 tile that's 24 in of joint per square foot.
2. **Volume** = joint length × joint width × tile thickness. 24 × 1/8 × 3/8 = 1.125 cubic inches per square foot.
3. **Weight**: we assume one pound of dry sanded cement grout fills about 12 cubic inches of joint once mixed. That's the low end of typical values, so our estimates lean toward a little extra.

Why the huge spread? Joint length per square foot depends on how many tiles there are. A square foot of 2 × 2 mosaic has 144 in of joint; a square foot of 24 × 24 tile has 12 in. Joint width scales the result directly, so going from 1/16 to 3/16 in triples the grout.

## Worked example: a bathroom

- **Floor:** 40 sq ft of 12 × 24 in porcelain, 3/8 in thick, 1/8 in joints, 10% extra: about **3.1 lb**, one 25 lb bag with plenty left over.
- **Shower walls:** 75 sq ft of 3 × 6 in subway tile, 5/16 in thick, 1/16 in joints, 10% extra: about **9.7 lb**. That's 1 bag of 10 lb unsanded grout, since joints under 1/8 in usually call for unsanded.

You can also run tile counts in the [tile calculator](/tile-calculator/); for how many boxes of tile to buy, see [how many tiles are in a box](/guides/how-many-tiles-in-a-box/).

## Sanded or unsanded?

- **Unsanded grout** for joints narrower than about 1/8 in, and for polished stone or glass tile that sand could scratch.
- **Sanded grout** for joints about 1/8 in and wider. The sand keeps it from shrinking and cracking in wider joints.
- **Epoxy and urethane grouts** for showers and kitchens where stain resistance matters. They cost more and cover differently.

Always check the grout maker's stated joint-width range.

## Tips

- **Mix in small batches.** Cement grout has a limited working time. Mix what you can use in 20 to 30 minutes.
- **Buy one color lot.** Grout color varies between batches, just like tile.
- **Keep a little extra** for repairs. A small bag of the same color makes future fixes invisible.
