**Pavers per square foot = 144 ÷ (paver width × paver length, in inches). Multiply by the patio's square footage and add 5% for a straight pattern or 10% or more for herringbone, curves and borders.** A 4 × 8 in brick paver works out to 4.5 per square foot; a 12 × 12 in paver to 1.

## Waste depends on the pattern and the edges

- **Running bond or basket weave** in a rectangle: about 5%. Cuts only happen at the edges.
- **Herringbone, 45° patterns and circles:** 10% or more. Every edge piece is cut at an angle.
- **Curved edges** add cuts along the whole curve; estimate them like a herringbone.
- **Borders** (a soldier course of pavers along the edge) are counted separately by length: a border of 4 × 8 pavers laid lengthwise takes 1.5 pavers per foot.

## Worked example: a 14 × 12 ft patio

6 × 9 in pavers in a herringbone pattern:

1. Area: 14 × 12 = **168 sq ft**.
2. Per sq ft: 144 ÷ (6 × 9) = **2.67**.
3. Pavers: 168 × 2.67 = 448, plus 10% = **493**.
4. Bedding sand at 1 in: **0.52 yd³**, or 28 bags of 0.5 cu ft.
5. Base, 4 in of compacted crusher run, extended 6 in past the edges: **2.77 yd³**, about 4.2 tons.
6. Edge restraint: the exposed perimeter, up to 2 × (14 + 12) = **52 ft**.

For the sand and base layers in detail, see [how much sand you need under pavers](/guides/how-much-sand-under-pavers/).

## Buying tips

- **Pavers are sold by the pallet or by the square foot.** A pallet covers a fixed area for each style; ask how much, and whether a partial pallet costs more per paver.
- **Blend from several pallets.** Color varies within a production run, so take pavers from two or three pallets at once as you lay.
- **Keep spares.** A few extra pavers make it easy to replace a stained or cracked one later.
