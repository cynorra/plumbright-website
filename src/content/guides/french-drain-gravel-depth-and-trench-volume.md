**To estimate French drain gravel volume, calculate the trench's cross-sectional area (width x total gravel depth) and multiply by the linear footage. For a typical 12-inch wide trench with 18 inches of gravel, expect approximately 0.167 cubic feet of gravel per linear foot, or 0.62 cubic yards per 100 linear feet, including a 10% waste factor. Actual requirements vary significantly with trench dimensions, pipe diameter, and desired gravel layers, necessitating a detailed calculation from the table below.**

## Calculating Trench Volume and Gravel Requirements

A properly constructed French drain relies on specific dimensions for effective water diversion. The total trench volume must accommodate the perforated drain pipe, a surrounding layer of permeable gravel, and a geotextile fabric liner. The calculation begins with the trench's overall dimensions, followed by determining the volume occupied by the gravel.

### Step-by-Step Volume Calculation:

1.  **Determine Trench Dimensions:**
    *   **Width (W):** Typical French drain trenches range from 6 to 18 inches wide. A common width is 12 inches to allow for adequate gravel around a 4-inch pipe.
    *   **Total Depth (D):** This includes the gravel base, the pipe, and the gravel cover above the pipe, plus any topsoil cap. Depths often range from 18 to 36 inches, depending on the drainage issue and frost line.
    *   **Length (L):** The total linear footage of the drain run.

2.  **Calculate Total Trench Cross-Sectional Area:**
    *   Area (sq ft) = (Width in inches / 12) \* (Total Depth in inches / 12)
    *   *Example:* For a 12-inch wide, 24-inch deep trench:
        *   Area = (12 in / 12) \* (24 in / 12) = 1 ft \* 2 ft = 2 square feet.

3.  **Calculate Total Trench Volume (Cubic Feet):**
    *   Volume (cu ft) = Area (sq ft) \* Length (L in feet)
    *   *Example:* For a 50-foot run of the above trench:
        *   Volume = 2 sq ft \* 50 ft = 100 cubic feet.

4.  **Determine Gravel-Specific Volume:**
    *   The gravel typically fills the trench from the bottom up to a few inches below the surface, leaving space for topsoil or sod.
    *   **Gravel Depth (GD):** This is the total depth of gravel, which includes the base layer, the space around the pipe, and the top layer. It's usually 2-6 inches less than the total trench depth to allow for a topsoil cap.
    *   **Gravel Cross-Sectional Area (GSA):**
        *   GSA (sq ft) = (Width in inches / 12) \* (Gravel Depth in inches / 12)
    *   **Gravel Volume (GV):**
        *   GV (cu ft) = GSA (sq ft) \* Length (L in feet)
    *   *Example:* For a 12-inch wide, 20-inch deep gravel fill in a 50-foot trench:
        *   GSA = (12 in / 12) \* (20 in / 12) = 1 ft \* 1.67 ft = 1.67 sq ft
        *   GV = 1.67 sq ft \* 50 ft = 83.5 cubic feet.

5.  **Account for Pipe Displacement (Optional but Recommended):**
    *   A 4-inch perforated drain pipe (common for residential French drains) has an outside diameter of approximately 4.5 inches.
    *   Volume of 4-inch pipe per linear foot:
        *   Radius (r) = 4.5 in / 2 = 2.25 inches = 0.1875 feet
        *   Volume per foot = π \* r² \* 1 ft = 3.14159 \* (0.1875 ft)² \* 1 ft ≈ 0.11 cubic feet per linear foot.
    *   For a 50-foot run, the pipe displaces 0.11 cu ft/ft \* 50 ft = 5.5 cubic feet of gravel.
    *   Subtract this from the total gravel volume: 83.5 cu ft - 5.5 cu ft = 78 cubic feet.

6.  **Apply Waste Factor:**
    *   Always add a 10% waste factor for settling, spillage, and uneven trench bottoms.
    *   Gravel Volume with Waste = GV \* 1.10
    *   *Example:* 78 cu ft \* 1.10 = 85.8 cubic feet.

7.  **Convert to Cubic Yards:**
    *   There are 27 cubic feet in 1 cubic yard.
    *   Cubic Yards = Cubic Feet / 27
    *   *Example:* 85.8 cu ft / 27 = 3.18 cubic yards.

For quick estimates, you can use our [gravel calculator](/gravel-calculator/). Remember that a minimum 1% slope (1 foot drop per 100 feet of run) is crucial for effective drainage, which may slightly alter effective depth along the run.

## Worked Project Example: 50-Foot Residential French Drain

Let's calculate the materials for a 50-foot long French drain designed to intercept surface and subsurface water near a foundation.

**Project Specifications:**

*   **Drain Length:** 50 linear feet
*   **Trench Width:** 12 inches
*   **Total Trench Depth:** 24 inches (allowing for a 4-inch topsoil cap)
*   **Gravel Type:** #57 washed stone
*   **Pipe:** 4-inch perforated drain pipe
*   **Geotextile Fabric:** Non-woven, 6 oz/sq yd

### Step-by-Step Material Calculation:

1.  **Calculate Total Trench Volume:**
    *   Trench Width (in feet) = 12 inches / 12 = 1 foot
    *   Total Trench Depth (in feet) = 24 inches / 12 = 2 feet
    *   Cross-sectional Area = 1 ft \* 2 ft = 2 sq ft
    *   Total Trench Volume = 2 sq ft \* 50 ft = 100 cubic feet

2.  **Determine Gravel Depth and Volume:**
    *   With a 24-inch total trench depth and a 4-inch topsoil cap, the gravel depth will be 20 inches.
    *   Gravel Depth (in feet) = 20 inches / 12 = 1.67 feet
    *   Gravel Cross-sectional Area = 1 ft (width) \* 1.67 ft (gravel depth) = 1.67 sq ft
    *   Initial Gravel Volume = 1.67 sq ft \* 50 ft = 83.5 cubic feet

3.  **Account for Pipe Displacement:**
    *   A 4-inch perforated pipe displaces approximately 0.11 cubic feet per linear foot.
    *   Pipe Displacement Volume = 0.11 cu ft/ft \* 50 ft = 5.5 cubic feet
    *   Adjusted Gravel Volume = 83.5 cu ft - 5.5 cu ft = 78 cubic feet

4.  **Apply Waste Factor:**
    *   Recommended 10% waste factor for gravel.
    *   Gravel Volume with Waste = 78 cu ft \* 1.10 = 85.8 cubic feet

5.  **Convert Gravel Volume to Cubic Yards:**
    *   Cubic Yards of Gravel = 85.8 cu ft / 27 cu ft/yd = **3.18 cubic yards** of #57 washed stone.
    *   When ordering, it's common practice to round up to the nearest half or full yard, so **3.5 cubic yards** would be a safe order. For more on gravel weights and ordering, refer to our guide: [Gravel Driveway Depth and Layers: How Much Stone to Order](/guides/gravel-driveway-depth-and-layers/).

6.  **Calculate Geotextile Fabric Needs:**
    *   The fabric needs to line the bottom and sides of the trench and have enough overlap to wrap over the top layer of gravel.
    *   Required Fabric Width = Trench Width + (2 \* Total Trench Depth) + Overlap
    *   Required Fabric Width = 12 in + (2 \* 24 in) + 12 in (for overlap) = 12 + 48 + 12 = 72 inches = 6 feet.
    *   Required Fabric Length = Drain Length + 10% for overlap/waste = 50 ft \* 1.10 = 55 feet.
    *   Therefore, you would need a roll of geotextile fabric that is at least **6 feet wide and 55 feet long**.

This detailed breakdown ensures all components are accounted for, minimizing material shortages and project delays. Remember to consider the 1% minimum slope for effective water flow, which means the trench depth will gradually increase along its run.
