**A 40 lb bag of standard concrete mix makes about 0.30 cubic feet, a 60 lb bag about 0.45 and an 80 lb bag about 0.60. It takes 90, 60 and 45 bags respectively to make one cubic yard.** Weight for weight, every size yields the same: about 133 lb of dry mix per cubic foot of concrete. The only real differences are price per cubic foot and how much you can lift.

## Comparing cost: divide by yield

Shelf prices vary by store and region, so compare bags by cost per cubic foot, not per bag:

**Cost per cubic foot = bag price ÷ bag yield**

Smaller bags usually cost more per cubic foot, because packaging and handling cost the same per bag. Past roughly a cubic yard, ready-mix delivered by truck is usually cheaper than any bag, and a lot less work.

## 80 lb bags: fewer trips, heavier lifts

The 80 lb bag is the default for a reason: fewer bags to buy, carry, open and mix. A 10 × 10 ft patio is 62 bags of 80 lb mix instead of 123 bags of 40 lb mix. But 80 lb is a heavy, awkward lift, repeated dozens of times. Consider:

- **40 or 50 lb bags** if you're carrying them far, up steps, or working alone and want to avoid back strain.
- **60 lb bags** as a middle ground; some stores stock them in more mix types.
- **80 lb bags** when you have a mixer, a helper and a wheelbarrow route.

## Mixing tips that affect yield

- **Use the water the bag calls for.** Extra water makes the mix easier to place but weaker, and it doesn't make more concrete.
- **Mix full bags.** Splitting a bag invites wrong ratios. If you need a half bag, mix it carefully by weight.
- **Fast-setting post mix** is poured dry and soaked in the hole. Its yield per bag can be lower than standard mix, so check the label before counting bags.

<div class="bg-note text-note-foreground my-6 rounded-lg px-4 py-3 text-sm" role="note">Wet concrete is caustic and can burn skin. Wear gloves, eye protection and long sleeves, and a dust mask when opening and pouring dry mix.</div>
