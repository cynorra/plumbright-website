**Estimating roofing underlayment requires calculating the total roof area in squares, then dividing by the *effective* coverage per roll, not the nominal coverage. Always factor in head-lap and side-lap requirements, which vary by material type (felt vs. synthetic) and roof pitch, plus a 10% waste allowance for cuts and overlaps, to ensure adequate material.** This guide provides precise formulas and a comprehensive table for accurate material acquisition.

## Understanding Underlayment Coverage: Felt vs. Synthetic

Roofing underlayment serves as a crucial secondary barrier against moisture intrusion, installed directly onto the roof deck before the final roofing material. The two primary types are asphalt-saturated felt (15 lb and 30 lb) and synthetic underlayment. While both provide protection, their roll dimensions, weight, installation methods, and effective coverage differ significantly, impacting material estimation.

**Nominal vs. Effective Coverage:**
*   **Nominal Coverage:** This is the total square footage advertised on the roll (e.g., a 4-square roll of 15 lb felt nominally covers 400 square feet).
*   **Effective Coverage:** This is the actual square footage a roll will cover *after* accounting for necessary overlaps (head-lap and side-lap) during installation. The effective coverage is always less than the nominal coverage.

**Lap Requirements:**
*   **Head-lap:** The amount by which an upper course of underlayment overlaps the course below it. This is critical for water shedding.
    *   **15 lb Felt:** Typically requires a 2-inch head-lap on standard slopes (4:12 pitch or greater).
    *   **30 lb Felt:** Typically requires a 4-inch head-lap on standard slopes.
    *   **Synthetic Underlayment:** Often requires a 2-inch head-lap on standard slopes, but may require 4-inch or 6-inch head-laps on low-slope roofs (2:12 to 4:12 pitch) or in high-wind areas, as specified by the manufacturer.
*   **Side-lap:** The amount by which one roll overlaps an adjacent roll laid horizontally.
    *   **Felt (15 lb & 30 lb):** Typically requires a 2-inch side-lap.
    *   **Synthetic Underlayment:** Typically requires a 3-inch side-lap.

Ignoring these lap requirements will lead to underestimation and potential material shortages during installation. Additionally, a **10% waste allowance** is standard practice to account for cuts, trimming, damaged material, and unexpected overlaps, ensuring enough material is on hand for the entire project.

## Calculating Underlayment Rolls: The Step-by-Step Method

Accurately determining the number of underlayment rolls involves a few key steps:

1.  **Calculate Total Roof Area in Square Feet:**
    *   Measure the length and width of each roof plane.
    *   Multiply length by width for each plane to get its square footage.
    *   For pitched roofs, you must account for the slope. Multiply the flat footprint area by the appropriate roof pitch factor. For example, a 6/12 pitch has a factor of 1.118. You can find these factors using a [Roof Pitch Factor Chart & Multiplier: Slopes from 1/12 to 18/12](/guides/roof-pitch-multiplier-chart/).
    *   Sum the square footage of all roof planes to get the total roof area.

2.  **Convert Total Roof Area to "Squares":**
    *   A "square" in roofing terminology equals 100 square feet.
    *   Divide your total roof area in square feet by 100.
    *   *Formula:* `Total Roof Area (sq ft) / 100 = Total Squares`

3.  **Determine Effective Coverage Per Roll:**
    *   Refer to the "Effective Sq Ft per Roll" in the table below for your chosen underlayment type. This value already accounts for standard head-laps and side-laps.

4.  **Calculate Base Number of Rolls Needed:**
    *   Divide the total roof area in square feet by the effective coverage per roll.
    *   *Formula:* `Total Roof Area (sq ft) / Effective Sq Ft per Roll = Base Rolls Needed`

5.  **Apply Waste Allowance:**
    *   Multiply the base number of rolls by 1.10 (for a 10% waste factor).
    *   *Formula:* `Base Rolls Needed * 1.10 = Rolls with Waste`

6.  **Round Up to the Nearest Whole Roll:**
    *   You cannot purchase partial rolls. Always round up to the next whole number to ensure you have enough material.

## Worked Project Example: Estimating Underlayment for a Gable Roof

Let's calculate the underlayment needed for a house with a 28 ft x 40 ft footprint and a 7/12 roof pitch.

**1. Calculate Total Roof Area in Square Feet:**
*   Footprint Area = 28 ft * 40 ft = 1120 sq ft
*   For a 7/12 pitch, the roof pitch factor is approximately 1.167 (from a roof pitch multiplier chart).
*   Total Roof Area = Footprint Area * Pitch Factor = 1120 sq ft * 1.167 = 1307.04 sq ft

**2. Convert Total Roof Area to "Squares":**
*   Total Squares = 1307.04 sq ft / 100 = 13.07 squares

**Scenario A: Using 15 lb Felt Underlayment**

*   **3. Determine Effective Coverage Per Roll:** From the table, 15 lb felt has an effective coverage of 380 sq ft per roll.
*   **4. Calculate Base Number of Rolls Needed:**
    *   Base Rolls = Total Roof Area / Effective Sq Ft per Roll
    *   Base Rolls = 1307.04 sq ft / 380 sq ft/roll = 3.439 rolls
*   **5. Apply Waste Allowance:**
    *   Rolls with Waste = 3.439 rolls * 1.10 = 3.783 rolls
*   **6. Round Up to the Nearest Whole Roll:**
    *   You would need **4 rolls of 15 lb felt** underlayment.

**Scenario B: Using Common 10-Square Synthetic Underlayment**

*   **3. Determine Effective Coverage Per Roll:** From the table, common 10-square synthetic underlayment has an effective coverage of 920 sq ft per roll (assuming standard laps for a 7/12 pitch).
*   **4. Calculate Base Number of Rolls Needed:**
    *   Base Rolls = Total Roof Area / Effective Sq Ft per Roll
    *   Base Rolls = 1307.04 sq ft / 920 sq ft/roll = 1.421 rolls
*   **5. Apply Waste Allowance:**
    *   Rolls with Waste = 1.421 rolls * 1.10 = 1.563 rolls
*   **6. Round Up to the Nearest Whole Roll:**
    *   You would need **2 rolls of common 10-square synthetic underlayment**.

As demonstrated, synthetic underlayment typically covers a much larger area per roll, reducing the number of rolls required and potentially speeding up installation due to fewer seams. For further material estimates, including how many bundles of shingles in a square, refer to our detailed guides.
