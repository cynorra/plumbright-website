**There's no standard number of tiles in a box. The label on the box lists both the number of pieces and the square feet they cover, and those are the numbers to plan with.** As a rough guide, one 12 × 12 in tile covers 1 sq ft, a 12 × 24 in tile covers 2 sq ft and a 24 × 24 in tile covers 4 sq ft; boxes are packed so they stay liftable, which is why large tiles come only a few to a box.

## Converting between pieces and square feet

If a box shows only one of the two numbers, you can work out the other:

- **Sq ft per box = pieces × tile width (in) × tile length (in) ÷ 144**
- **Pieces per box = sq ft per box ÷ (width × length ÷ 144)**

A box of 8 pieces of 12 × 24 in tile covers 8 × 12 × 24 ÷ 144 = **16 sq ft** by face area. If the box says 15.5 sq ft, the tiles are slightly under nominal size, which is common. Trust the printed figure.

## The grout joint makes a box go further

Box coverage is based on the tile faces. On the floor, every tile also "owns" half the grout joint around it, so each tile covers a little more than its face. The smaller the tile, the bigger the effect:

## Worked example: count boxes two ways

An 85 sq ft floor in 12 × 24 in tile, 1/8 in joints, 10% waste. The box says 8 pieces, 15.5 sq ft.

- **By pieces:** you need 47 tiles. 47 ÷ 8 = 5.88 → **6 boxes**.
- **By square feet:** 85 × 1.10 = 93.5 sq ft ÷ 15.5 = 6.03 → **7 boxes**.

The two methods differ by a box, because the piece count includes the grout joint bonus and the square-foot method doesn't. When in doubt, buy the higher number.

## Buying tips

- **Check the lot and shade number on every box.** Tiles from different production runs can differ visibly.
- **Open a box before you leave the store** if you can, to check for breakage and the actual size.
- **Ask about returns.** Many stores take back unopened boxes, which makes rounding up nearly free.
- **Mosaic sheets** are sold by the sheet, with the sheet size (often about 12 × 12 in) and sq ft per carton on the label. Count sheets, not individual tiles.
