**Measure the room, multiply length × width for square feet and divide by 9 for square yards. Then remember that carpet is cut from a roll, usually 12 ft wide, so you pay for full-width pieces plus a few inches of trim at each end.** A 12 × 15 ft room is 20 sq yd, but the piece you buy is 12 ft × 15.5 ft = **20.67 sq yd**.

Rooms up to 12 ft wide fit one piece with very little waste. Past 12 ft, a second full-width piece is needed, so a 13 × 14 ft room buys almost twice its area. The [carpet calculator](/carpet-calculator/) works out the best direction, seams and waste for any room, and adds pad and tack strip.

## How to measure

1. **Measure at the widest points,** wall to wall, into doorways to the middle of the door jamb where the carpet will end.
2. **Include closets** that get carpet. A closet is often covered by an offcut from the main piece if the shapes line up; ask your installer.
3. **Round each measurement up** to the next inch or two.
4. **Sketch the room** with the measurements and the direction of the main window and door. Seams go where they'll show least.

## Worked example: two bedrooms

An 11 × 12 ft bedroom and a 10 × 10 ft bedroom, 12 ft roll:

- Bedroom 1: one piece 12 × 11.5 ft = **15.33 sq yd** for a 14.67 sq yd room. The extra foot of width is trimmed off.
- Bedroom 2: one piece 12 × 10.5 ft = **14 sq yd** for 11.11 sq yd.

The 2 ft strip trimmed off the 10 ft wide room is 2 × 10.5 ft: enough for a closet floor, if it's in the same carpet and runs the same way.

## Pad and tack strip

- **Pad** is bought close to room size, since it has no pattern or pile direction and seams in it don't show. Room area + 5% covers trimming.
- **Tack strip** goes around the perimeter, except across doorways: a 12 × 15 ft room with one door needs about 51 ft.
- **Transitions**: a metal or wood strip where carpet meets another floor at each doorway.

## Comparing quotes

Retailers usually measure for free and price from their own seam plan, often including pad, installation and furniture moving. If a quote is much higher than your calculation, ask to see the seam diagram: extra square yards usually come from a seam plan that's easier to install, from matching a pattern, or from stairs.
