**For a durable paver installation, excavate a total depth that accommodates the paver thickness, a 1-inch bedding sand layer, and a 4-6 inch compacted crushed stone base.** This typically results in an excavation depth of 7 to 9 inches for common 2-inch to 3-inch thick pavers, ensuring proper drainage and structural integrity. Always account for a 10% waste factor when ordering materials.

## How the Formula Works

Accurately determining the excavation depth and material quantities for a paver base is crucial for a long-lasting installation. The total excavation depth is the sum of three components: the paver thickness, the bedding sand layer, and the compacted crushed stone base.

**1. Total Excavation Depth Calculation:**
The first step is to calculate the total depth required for your project.
*   **Total Excavation Depth (inches) = Paver Thickness (inches) + Bedding Sand Depth (inches) + Compacted Crushed Stone Base Depth (inches)**

    *   **Paver Thickness:** This varies by paver type, commonly 2 inches for walkways and patios, or 2.5-3 inches for driveways.
    *   **Bedding Sand Depth:** A uniform 1 inch of coarse bedding sand (e.g., concrete sand or C-33 washed sand) is standard for setting pavers.
    *   **Compacted Crushed Stone Base Depth:** For most residential patios and walkways, a 4-inch compacted base of crushed stone (such as quarry process or ¾-inch minus gravel) is sufficient. For driveways or heavy-traffic areas, increase this to 6-8 inches.

**2. Crushed Stone (Base) Volume Calculation:**
Once the depths are established, calculate the volume of crushed stone needed. This material is typically sold by the cubic yard.

*   **Step 2a: Calculate Area (Square Feet)**
    *   `Area (sq ft) = Length (ft) × Width (ft)`

*   **Step 2b: Convert Base Depth to Feet**
    *   `Compacted Base Depth (ft) = Compacted Base Depth (inches) ÷ 12`

*   **Step 2c: Calculate Cubic Feet of Crushed Stone**
    *   `Volume (cu ft) = Area (sq ft) × Compacted Base Depth (ft)`

*   **Step 2d: Apply Waste Factor and Convert to Cubic Yards**
    *   `Total Crushed Stone (cu yds) = (Volume (cu ft) × 1.10) ÷ 27`
        *   *Note:* The 1.10 factor accounts for a 10% waste allowance due to compaction, spillage, and uneven subgrade. There are 27 cubic feet in 1 cubic yard.

**3. Bedding Sand Volume Calculation:**
Similar to the crushed stone, calculate the volume of bedding sand needed.

*   **Step 3a: Calculate Area (Square Feet)**
    *   `Area (sq ft) = Length (ft) × Width (ft)` (This will be the same as for the crushed stone base).

*   **Step 3b: Convert Sand Depth to Feet**
    *   `Bedding Sand Depth (ft) = Bedding Sand Depth (inches) ÷ 12` (For 1 inch, this is 1/12 or approximately 0.0833 ft).

*   **Step 3c: Calculate Cubic Feet of Bedding Sand**
    *   `Volume (cu ft) = Area (sq ft) × Bedding Sand Depth (ft)`

*   **Step 3d: Apply Waste Factor and Convert to Cubic Yards**
    *   `Total Bedding Sand (cu yds) = (Volume (cu ft) × 1.10) ÷ 27`
        *   *Note:* Again, the 1.10 factor accounts for a 10% waste allowance. For more detailed sand calculations, refer to our guide on [how much sand do you need under pavers](/guides/how-much-sand-under-pavers/).

## Worked Project Example

Let's calculate the excavation depth and material needs for a 12 ft x 20 ft patio using 2.5-inch thick pavers. We will use a 4-inch compacted crushed stone base and a 1-inch bedding sand layer.

**Project Specifications:**
*   Area: 12 ft x 20 ft
*   Paver Thickness: 2.5 inches
*   Compacted Crushed Stone Base: 4 inches
*   Bedding Sand: 1 inch

**Step 1: Calculate Total Excavation Depth**
*   Total Excavation Depth = Paver Thickness + Bedding Sand Depth + Compacted Crushed Stone Base Depth
*   Total Excavation Depth = 2.5 inches + 1 inch + 4 inches = **7.5 inches**

**Step 2: Calculate Area**
*   Area = Length × Width
*   Area = 12 ft × 20 ft = **240 sq ft**

**Step 3: Calculate Crushed Stone (Base) Volume**
*   Convert Base Depth to Feet: 4 inches ÷ 12 = 0.3333 ft
*   Volume (cu ft) = Area × Compacted Base Depth (ft)
*   Volume (cu ft) = 240 sq ft × 0.3333 ft = 79.992 cu ft
*   Apply Waste Factor and Convert to Cubic Yards: (79.992 cu ft × 1.10) ÷ 27
*   Total Crushed Stone = 87.9912 cu ft ÷ 27 = **3.26 cubic yards**

**Step 4: Calculate Bedding Sand Volume**
*   Convert Sand Depth to Feet: 1 inch ÷ 12 = 0.0833 ft
*   Volume (cu ft) = Area × Bedding Sand Depth (ft)
*   Volume (cu ft) = 240 sq ft × 0.0833 ft = 19.992 cu ft
*   Apply Waste Factor and Convert to Cubic Yards: (19.992 cu ft × 1.10) ÷ 27
*   Total Bedding Sand = 21.9912 cu ft ÷ 27 = **0.81 cubic yards**

**Summary for 12 ft x 20 ft Patio:**
*   **Total Excavation Depth:** 7.5 inches
*   **Crushed Stone (Quarry Process):** Approximately 3.3 cubic yards
*   **Bedding Sand:** Approximately 0.9 cubic yards

## Setting Tips and Waste Allowance

**Waste Allowance:** A 10% waste factor is a standard and realistic buffer for both crushed stone and bedding sand. This accounts for several common scenarios:
*   **Compaction:** Materials compact more than initially estimated, especially crushed stone.
*   **Uneven Subgrade:** The excavated area may not be perfectly level, requiring more material in certain spots.
*   **Spillage:** Some material is inevitably lost during transport, spreading, and leveling.
*   **Future Needs:** Having a small surplus can be useful for minor repairs or adjustments.

**Compaction is Key:** The crushed stone base must be thoroughly compacted in lifts (layers) of no more than 4 inches using a plate compactor. Proper compaction prevents settling and ensures a stable foundation for your pavers. Inadequate compaction is a primary cause of paver failure.

**Drainage Considerations:** The crushed stone base, particularly quarry process, provides excellent drainage, allowing water to permeate through the paver system and into the subgrade. Ensure the subgrade itself is sloped away from any structures at a minimum of 1/8 inch per foot to prevent water pooling. For more information on material weights, consult our guide on [how much does a cubic yard of gravel weigh](/guides/how-much-does-a-cubic-yard-of-gravel-weigh/).

**Edge Restraints:** After the base and sand are laid, but before pavers are fully installed, consider installing rigid edge restraints around the perimeter of your paved area. These are critical for preventing the pavers from shifting or spreading over time, maintaining the integrity of your design.
