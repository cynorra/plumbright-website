**Crushed stone (angular gravel with sharp edges) interlocks and compacts into a firm, drivable surface that stays in place under car tires and snowplows, whereas pea gravel (rounded, smooth river rock) constantly shifts like ball bearings under foot and vehicle traffic.** For driveways and sloped paths, crushed stone (#411 or crusher run) is required; pea gravel is strictly suited for flat garden walkways, decorative borders, or playground bedding.

## Angular vs Rounded Aggregate: The Engineering Difference

The mechanical performance of gravel depends on how the rock particles were formed:

- **Crushed Stone (Angular Aggregate):** Mechanically fractured rock (limestone, granite, or traprock). The sharp, irregular faces interlock tightly when compacted with a plate compactor or heavy roller. Fines fill the voids, creating an almost concrete-like hardpack that resists wheel rutting.
- **Pea Gravel (Rounded Aggregate):** Naturally tumbled in rivers or glacial deposits (sizes 3/8 to 5/8 in). Because the stones are smooth and round with no fines, they cannot interlock or compact. They shift continuously under load, sink under car tires, and wash out on slopes exceeding 3 degrees.

## Step-by-Step Walkway Installation Rules

To install either material without migration:

1. **Excavation Depth:** Dig 4 inches deep for pedestrian walkways. For driveways using crushed stone, excavate 10 to 12 inches to allow a coarse #3 base layer.
2. **Edging Restraints:** Install rigid steel, aluminum, or heavy timber landscape edging standing 1/2 inch above the finished stone surface. Without edging, both crushed stone and pea gravel spread into lawn grass within weeks.
3. **Underlayment:** Lay non-woven geotextile fabric. Avoid thin plastic weed barrier, which tears under stone pressure.
4. **Volume Formula:** Length (ft) × Width (ft) × Depth (ft) ÷ 27 = Cubic Yards. Multiply cubic yards by 1.35 to get tons.

## Snow Removal and Seasonal Maintenance Differences

Winter maintenance is one of the most frequently overlooked factors when choosing between crushed stone and pea gravel:

- **Plowing and Snow Blowing:** Compacted crushed stone (#411 or crusher run) freezes into a rigid, monolithic slab during cold weather. A snowplow with a rubber cutting edge or a snowblower set 1/2 inch off the deck glides over the surface without throwing rocks into the lawn. Pea gravel, by contrast, never freezes solid; snowblower augers pull loose round pebbles into the impeller and throw them like shrapnel, ruining lawns and denting car doors.
- **Raking and Leaf Clearing:** In autumn, clearing leaves from crushed stone is straightforward with a leaf blower held at a low angle. Pea gravel blows away along with dry leaves, requiring constant raking and seasonal replenishment.
- **Stabilization Grid Alternative:** If you love the aesthetic appeal of smooth pea gravel for patios or light driving pads, consider installing permeable honeycomb stabilizer grids (such as TRUEGRID or CORE Gravel). These plastic cellular pavers lock the round pebbles into place, preventing sinking and rutting while preserving natural surface drainage.
