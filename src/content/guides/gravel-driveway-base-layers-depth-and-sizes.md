**A durable gravel driveway relies on a robust three-layer system: a coarse #3 stone base (4-6 inches), a mid-grade #57 stone layer (3-4 inches), and a fine crusher run top coat (2-3 inches).** Achieving proper depth and compaction for each layer is critical for longevity, requiring precise excavation and material calculations, including a 10% waste factor, to prevent premature rutting and ensure adequate drainage.

## Understanding the 3-Layer Gravel Driveway System

A well-constructed gravel driveway isn't just a pile of rocks; it's a carefully engineered system designed to distribute weight, manage water, and provide a stable driving surface. This typically involves three distinct layers of crushed stone, each serving a specific purpose:

1.  **Base Layer: #3 Stone (2-3 inch diameter)**
    *   **Purpose:** This is the foundational layer, providing excellent drainage and distributing heavy loads over the subgrade. Its large, angular stones interlock to create a stable, non-compactable base that prevents the finer materials from sinking into the soil.
    *   **Recommended Depth:** 4 to 6 inches (compacted).

2.  **Intermediate Layer: #57 Stone (1/2-1 inch diameter)**
    *   **Purpose:** The #57 stone fills the larger voids in the #3 base layer, creating a smoother, more compactable surface for the top layer. It continues to provide good drainage while adding stability and preventing the top layer from migrating downwards.
    *   **Recommended Depth:** 3 to 4 inches (compacted).

3.  **Surface Layer: Crusher Run (fines to 3/4 inch diameter)**
    *   **Purpose:** Also known as "dense grade aggregate" or "traffic bond," crusher run is a mix of crushed stone and stone dust. When compacted, the fines bind the larger pieces together, creating a tight, durable, and relatively dust-free driving surface that resists erosion and rutting.
    *   **Recommended Depth:** 2 to 3 inches (compacted).

## Calculating Gravel Tonnage for Each Layer

Estimating the correct amount of gravel involves calculating the volume for each layer and then converting that volume into tonnage. Always account for a waste factor to cover compaction, unevenness, and minor spillage.

**Step-by-Step Formula:**

1.  **Calculate Volume in Cubic Feet (cu ft):**
    *   `Volume (cu ft) = Length (feet) × Width (feet) × Desired Compacted Depth (feet)`
    *   *Note:* Convert inches to feet for depth (e.g., 6 inches = 0.5 feet).

2.  **Convert to Cubic Yards (cu yds):**
    *   `Volume (cu yds) = Volume (cu ft) / 27` (Since 1 cubic yard = 27 cubic feet)

3.  **Calculate Tonnage:**
    *   `Tonnage = Volume (cu yds) × Material Density (tons/cu yd)`
    *   *Standard Density:* Crushed gravel typically weighs around 1.4 tons per cubic yard. This can vary slightly by stone type and moisture content, but 1.4 tons/cu yd is a reliable average for estimation. For more detailed information, see our guide on [How Much Does a Cubic Yard of Gravel Weigh?](/guides/how-much-does-a-cubic-yard-of-gravel-weigh/).

4.  **Add Waste Allowance:**
    *   `Total Tonnage (with waste) = Tonnage × 1.10` (for a 10% waste factor)
    *   A 10% waste factor is recommended to account for compaction, minor grading adjustments, and material loss during delivery and spreading.
