**Space deck joists 16 in on center for 5/4 wood boards and most composite boards laid square to the joists. Use 12 in for diagonal decking and for composites that call for it. 24 in works only with 2× (1 1/2 in thick) wood decking or products rated for it.** The deck boards set the spacing; the joist size then has to span the distance from ledger to beam at that spacing.

## IRC limits for deck boards

From the 2021 International Residential Code, Table R507.7, maximum joist spacing for wood decking:

<div class="table-wrap">
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Decking</th><th>Square to joists</th><th>Diagonal (45°)</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>5/4 wood (1 1/4 in)</td><td>16 in</td><td>12 in</td></tr>
<tr><td>2x wood (2 in)</td><td>24 in</td><td>24 in</td></tr>
</tbody>
<caption>Multiple-span values: each board crosses three or more joists. Single-span boards have lower limits. Composite and PVC boards: follow the span on the product's code evaluation report.</caption>
</table>
</div>

A diagonal board crosses the joists at an angle, so the distance it spans between joists is longer than the joist spacing. That's why diagonal layouts need joists closer together.

## Joist span depends on spacing too

Closer joists each carry less load, so they can span farther. For southern pine joists at a 40 psf live load, the same code lists:

<div class="table-wrap">
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Joist</th><th>12 in o.c.</th><th>16 in o.c.</th><th>24 in o.c.</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>2x6</td><td>9'-11"</td><td>9'-0"</td><td>7'-7"</td></tr>
<tr><td>2x8</td><td>13'-1"</td><td>11'-10"</td><td>9'-8"</td></tr>
<tr><td>2x10</td><td>16'-2"</td><td>14'-0"</td><td>11'-5"</td></tr>
<tr><td>2x12</td><td>18'-0"</td><td>16'-6"</td><td>13'-6"</td></tr>
</tbody>
<caption>IRC 2021 Table R507.6, as printed in Anne Arundel County's deck guide. Other species span less. Cantilevers and heavy loads change the numbers.</caption>
</table>
</div>

So the choice is a trade-off. A 12 ft span from ledger to beam needs 2 × 10s at 16 in, but at 12 in on center a 2 × 8 would work (13 ft 1 in). More joists of a smaller size, or fewer of a larger size.

## How spacing changes the joist count

For a deck 12 ft deep (joists 12 ft long), by deck length along the house:

<div class="table-wrap">
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Deck length</th><th>Joists at 12 in</th><th>Joists at 16 in</th><th>Joists at 24 in</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>10 ft</td><td>11</td><td>8</td><td>6</td></tr>
<tr><td>12 ft</td><td>13</td><td>10</td><td>7</td></tr>
<tr><td>16 ft</td><td>17</td><td>13</td><td>9</td></tr>
<tr><td>20 ft</td><td>21</td><td>16</td><td>11</td></tr>
<tr><td>24 ft</td><td>25</td><td>19</td><td>13</td></tr>
</tbody>
<caption>Joists = length (in) ÷ spacing + 1. Rim joists and doubled joists at board seams are extra.</caption>
</table>
</div>

For a 16 × 12 ft deck, going from 16 in to 12 in spacing adds 4 joists, and the screw count rises from about 676 to 884, since every board is fastened at every joist. ## Which spacing should you choose?

- **16 in:** the default for most decks with 5/4 wood or composite boards laid straight.
- **12 in:** diagonal or herringbone decking, most composite boards on stairs, and when you want a stiffer, less bouncy feel. Many composite makers specify 12 in for stair treads and diagonal installs.
- **24 in:** only with 2× wood decking or boards rated for it. Saves joists but feels springier.

Ask the board maker, not just the code: composite and PVC decking warranties often depend on following their span rating, and those ratings sometimes drop in hot climates or for diagonal layouts.
